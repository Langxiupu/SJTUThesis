\begin{abstract}[zh]
在 IMT-2030 第六代移动通信（6G）体系中，低轨（Low Earth Orbit，LEO）卫星网络正从补充覆盖系统演变为支撑非地面接入、星间回传与全球业务承载的空间基础设施。再生式载荷与星间链路的成熟使 LEO 星座的网络管控从地面集中配置转向星上自治决策。但大规模 LEO 星座面临拓扑高速变化、业务时空分布非均匀、星间回传容量受限以及星上资源有限等约束，传统依赖全网状态汇聚和集中式调度的管控方式难以直接应用。6G 空天地一体化网络要求 LEO 星座具备分布式资源管控与用户级服务质量（Quality of Service，QoS）保障能力。

用户级 QoS 保障依赖网络状态估计、接入控制、资源调度与跨层传输的协同，需同时满足通信可靠性、资源分配效率与接入公平性。星上分布式管控条件下存在四类问题：退化遥测破坏本地链路负载与拓扑视图并使低可信状态扩散；多重覆盖与高动态可见关系使弱覆盖用户难以维持接入服务持续可用性；切片层预算对未来用户级 QoS 的支撑能力难以预测；星上轻量化语义传输难以平衡编码器复杂度、推理时延与传输性能。本文提出面向大规模 LEO 星座的分布式协同资源管控方法，以本地状态校准、有限邻域信令交互和跨节点协作替代全网同步与集中式求解，设计了四项技术：多源置信度调控的分布式网络状态感知、基于局部公平性估计的分布式协作接入切换、基于生成式网络状态感知的切片--MAC 分层协作优化，以及基于任务反馈与协同增量重传的轻量化语义传输。

在网络状态感知方面，本文提出多源置信度调控的分布式网络状态感知框架（CA-LDP）。该框架将本地状态感知分解为状态校准与置信度约束传播两阶段。状态校准阶段联合利用译码观测、时序先验、邻域空间支撑和信息新鲜度对退化遥测进行置信度自适应图信号修复；传播阶段通过置信度加权聚合限制低可信状态的传播强度。在 1584 颗卫星的 Starlink 星座及九类受控遥测退化场景下，CA-LDP 相比基线方法将平均传输速率提升 $4.8\%$--$10.8\%$，端到端时延降低 $6.7\%$--$11.1\%$，路由成功率接近 $100\%$。

在用户接入切换方面，本文提出基于局部公平性估计的分布式协作接入切换机制（FG-MAPPO）。在线切换时，FG-MAPPO 将用户长期累计接入效用作为邻域信令，使终端能低开销感知邻域长期服务状态。训练时采用广义 Gini 公平聚合函数构造公平性修正训练信号，通过吞吐优先--公平修正（TOFO）分立结构在保留链路质量判断的同时抑制长期服务分化。该机制兼容 3GPP 条件切换（CHO）流程，仅依赖本地观测和有限邻域信令联合考虑候选卫星的链路质量、负载状态和长期公平性。仿真表明，在仅交互邻域信息时，FG-MAPPO 的广义 Gini 公平性指标较集中式规划提升 $3.48\%$，平均速率提升 $6.91\%$。相比分布式强化学习基线，切换成功率与负载均衡性能最高分别提升 $15.65\%$ 与 $30.91\%$，邻域信令开销仅 $7.73$ kB/s。

在无线资源分配方面，本文提出基于生成式网络状态感知的切片--MAC 分层协作优化框架（DTPS）。该框架以带宽与功率预算为跨层接口，将多星流级资源优化分解为切片预算与时隙级流调度子问题。切片层基于生成式网络状态感知学习候选预算下未来 QoS 演化关系，压缩 MAC 层 QoS 满足所需的资源包络；MAC 层估计候选流满足速率--时延门限的最小资源开销，筛除不可满足流并执行 QCI 优先级加权选择。Starlink 规模星座仿真中，DTPS 在过载业务下实现 $80.8\%$ 的用户级 QoS 满足率，而 TDRL-RSUA、RL-JRSS、LACO 与 DQN-MAC 等基线方法的 QoS 满足率为 $33.0\%$--$56.3\%$，DTPS 平均时延保持在 $78.3$ ms。当突发不确定性增强时，QoS 满足率维持在 $97.6\%$ 以上。

在语义可靠传输方面，本文提出基于任务反馈与协同增量重传的轻量化语义传输机制（CIS-HARQ）。该机制将可靠性增益从模型复杂度扩展转向通信轮次与反馈协作，将完整重传重构为任务反馈引导的选择性增量编码与跨轮合并。接收端根据当前语义状态与任务特征差距生成反馈，发射端依据反馈生成受码率约束的语义增量，接收端逐轮恢复任务相关特征。在 ImageNet-100、CIFAR-100 与 VeRi-776 数据集、Rician 衰落信道、信噪比 $5$ dB 且信道带宽比（CBR）约 $0.02$ 的紧带宽条件下，CIS-HARQ 经三轮增量传输将下游任务精度由同带宽基线的 $35.7\%$ 提升至 $65.0\%$。该机制以约 $2.2$ M 参数与毫秒级星上推理时延，参数量较生成式扩散接收端减少约 $2500$ 倍，任务精度差距控制在约 $7\%$ 以内。
\end{abstract}

\begin{abstract}[en]
Under the IMT-2030 vision of sixth-generation (6G) mobile communications, low Earth orbit (LEO) satellite networks are evolving from complementary coverage systems into space infrastructure for non-terrestrial access, inter-satellite backhaul, and globally continuous services. The maturity of regenerative payloads and inter-satellite links enables network management in LEO constellations to shift from ground-centric centralized configuration to onboard autonomous decision-making. However, large-scale LEO constellations face constraints such as rapidly varying topology, nonuniform spatiotemporal traffic distribution, constrained inter-satellite backhaul capacity, and limited onboard resources. Conventional management paradigms based on global-state collection and centralized scheduling are difficult to apply directly. 6G space-air-ground integrated networks require LEO constellations to possess distributed resource management and user-level quality-of-service (QoS) assurance capabilities.

User-level QoS assurance depends on coordination among network-state estimation, access control, resource scheduling, and cross-layer transmission, requiring simultaneous satisfaction of communication reliability, resource-allocation efficiency, and access fairness. Under onboard distributed management conditions, four types of problems exist: degraded telemetry disrupts local link-load and topology views and spreads low-confidence states; multiple coverage and highly dynamic visibility relationships make it difficult for weak-coverage users to maintain continuous access-service availability; the capability of slice-layer budgets to support future user-level QoS is hard to predict; and onboard lightweight semantic transmission struggles to balance encoder complexity, inference latency, and transmission performance. This dissertation proposes a distributed cooperative resource-management method for large-scale LEO constellations, replacing global-state synchronization and centralized scheduling with local state calibration, limited neighborhood signaling exchange, and cross-node cooperation. Four techniques are designed: distributed network-state awareness with multi-source confidence regulation, distributed cooperative access-handover based on local fairness estimation, slice--MAC hierarchical cooperative optimization based on generative network-state awareness, and lightweight semantic transmission based on task feedback and coordinated incremental retransmission.

For network-state awareness, this dissertation proposes a distributed network-state awareness framework with multi-source confidence regulation (CA-LDP). CA-LDP decomposes local state awareness into two stages: state calibration and confidence-constrained propagation. The state-calibration stage jointly exploits decoded observations, temporal priors, neighborhood spatial support, and information freshness to recover degraded telemetry through confidence-adaptive graph-signal restoration; the propagation stage uses confidence-weighted aggregation to limit the propagation strength of low-confidence states. In a 1584-satellite Starlink constellation and nine controlled telemetry-degradation scenarios, CA-LDP improves the average transmission rate by $4.8\%$--$10.8\%$ and reduces end-to-end delay by $6.7\%$--$11.1\%$ compared to baseline methods, with routing success ratio close to $100\%$.

For user access-handover, this dissertation proposes a distributed cooperative access-handover mechanism based on local fairness estimation (FG-MAPPO). During online handover, FG-MAPPO uses each user's long-term cumulative access utility as neighborhood signaling, enabling terminals to sense the long-term service state of the neighborhood with low overhead. During training, this method employs generalized Gini fairness aggregation to construct fairness-corrected training signals and uses the throughput-oriented and fairness-oriented (TOFO) structure to preserve candidate-link quality judgment while suppressing long-term service differentiation. The mechanism is compatible with the 3GPP Conditional Handover (CHO) process and jointly considers candidate-link quality, satellite load, and long-term fairness using only local observations and limited neighborhood signaling. Simulations show that with only neighborhood information exchange, FG-MAPPO improves the generalized Gini fairness metric by $3.48\%$ and the average rate by $6.91\%$ compared to a centralized planning reference. Compared with distributed reinforcement-learning baselines, it improves the handover success ratio and load-balancing performance by up to $15.65\%$ and $30.91\%$, respectively, with a negligible neighborhood signaling overhead of only $7.73$ kB/s.

For radio-resource allocation, this dissertation proposes a slice--MAC hierarchical cooperative optimization framework based on generative network-state awareness (DTPS). With bandwidth and power budgets as the cross-layer interface, DTPS decomposes the multi-satellite flow-level resource optimization into slice-budget and slot-level flow-scheduling subproblems. At the slice layer, based on generative network-state awareness, DTPS learns the evolution of future QoS under candidate budgets, thereby compressing the resource envelope required for MAC-layer QoS satisfaction. At the MAC layer, DTPS estimates the minimum resource cost for each candidate flow to satisfy its rate--delay threshold, filters out unsatisfiable flows, and performs QCI-priority weighted selection. In Starlink-scale simulations, DTPS achieves a user-level QoS satisfaction ratio of $80.8\%$ under overloaded traffic, while baseline methods such as TDRL-RSUA, RL-JRSS, LACO, and DQN-MAC achieve QoS satisfaction ratios of $33.0\%$--$56.3\%$, and DTPS keeps the average delay at $78.3$ ms. When burst uncertainty increases, the QoS satisfaction ratio remains above $97.6\%$.

For reliable semantic transmission, this dissertation proposes a lightweight semantic transmission mechanism based on task feedback and coordinated incremental retransmission (CIS-HARQ). This mechanism shifts reliability gain from model-complexity expansion to communication rounds and feedback cooperation, reformulating full retransmission as task-feedback-guided selective incremental encoding and cross-round combining. The receiver generates feedback from the gap between the current semantic state and task features, the onboard transmitter generates rate-constrained semantic increments conditioned on this feedback, and the receiver progressively recovers task-relevant features. On the ImageNet-100, CIFAR-100, and VeRi-776 datasets over a Rician fading channel, at an SNR of $5$ dB and a tight channel bandwidth ratio (CBR) of about $0.02$, CIS-HARQ improves downstream task accuracy from $35.7\%$ for the equal-bandwidth baseline to $65.0\%$ after three incremental transmission rounds. With about $2.2$ M parameters and millisecond-level onboard inference latency, its parameter count is about $2500$ times lower than that of a generative diffusion receiver, while keeping the task-accuracy gap within about $7\%$.
\end{abstract}
