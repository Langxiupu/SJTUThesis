\begin{abstract}[zh]
在 IMT-2030 描绘的第六代移动通信（6G）体系中，低轨（Low Earth Orbit，LEO）卫星网络正由补充覆盖系统演进为支撑非地面接入、星间回传与全球连续业务承载的关键基础设施。随着再生式载荷、星间链路和星上处理能力逐步成熟，LEO 星座的网络管控模式正由地面集中配置转向星上自治决策与邻域协同。然而，大规模 LEO 星座具有拓扑高速变化、遥测信息不完备、回传链路受限以及星上计算与存储资源有限等特征，难以直接沿用地面蜂窝网络中依赖全局状态汇聚和集中式调度的管控范式。面向 6G 空天地一体化网络，LEO 星座需要从透明转发型覆盖系统进一步演进为具备端到端服务质量（Quality of Service，QoS）保障能力的核心承载网络。

端到端 QoS 保障并非由单一链路或单一协议层独立决定，而是依赖无线网络态势感知、用户接入、无线资源分配与业务传输等环节的协同。从全链路业务承载过程看，端到端 QoS 保障可分解为感知、接入、资源分配与传输四个子问题；从系统能力指标看，其核心在于同时支撑通信系统可靠性、资源分配效率与接入公平性。在星上分布式管控条件下，上述目标具体表现为四类关键挑战：退化遥测会削弱本地多跳网络视图的可信度，影响路由与资源决策的可靠性；海量用户在无中心协调条件下并发切换，使个体接入容量、服务连续性与系统级接入公平性相互制约；业务突发和信道波动使切片层预算与媒体接入控制（Medium Access Control，MAC）层调度难以及时协同，有限无线资源容易被分配给 QoS 目标难以满足的业务流；面向图像感知与识别等任务型业务，星上计算资源受限使语义传输难以简单依靠提升发射端编码模型表达能力或增强接收端恢复模型纠错能力来改善任务性能，受限链路下的任务相关信息传输需要同时满足传输可靠性、任务精度与星上开销约束。针对上述问题，本文提出面向大规模 LEO 星座端到端 QoS 保障的分布式协同资源管控方法，以本地状态校准、有限邻域交互和跨节点协作替代全局状态同步与集中式求解。具体来说，本文沿端到端业务承载路径设计多源置信度调控的分布式局部网络状态感知框架、基于局部公平性估计的分布式协作接入切换机制、基于生成式网络态势感知的切片--MAC 分层协作优化框架和基于任务反馈与协同增量重传的轻量化语义传输机制四项关键技术，由此形成从状态获取、接入建立、资源分配到业务传输的闭环保障体系。

（1）在网络状态感知方面，退化遥测会破坏本地多跳网络视图的可信性，使分布式路由缺少可靠状态输入。围绕这一问题，本文设计多源置信度调控的分布式局部网络状态感知框架，即置信度自适应局部分布式处理（Confidence-Adaptive Local Distributed Processing，CA-LDP）框架。该框架的创新点在于建立先校准、后聚合的可信状态感知机制：一方面，根据信息新鲜度、邻域空间相关性和当前观测质量，对陈旧与缺失遥测进行置信度自适应图信号修复；另一方面，以图反应--扩散模型刻画多跳状态传播，并通过受置信度调制的图信号聚合抑制低置信节点对可靠节点视图的污染。由此，分布式路由能够在不依赖全局状态汇聚的情况下获得可信的局部网络视图。在由 1584 颗卫星构成的 Starlink 真实星座、九类受控遥测退化场景下，CA-LDP 相较代表性基线将平均传输速率提升 $4.8\%$--$10.8\%$，端到端时延降低 $6.7\%$--$11.1\%$，并维持接近 $100\%$ 的路由成功率，表明其能够解决退化遥测条件下分布式路由缺少可靠状态输入的问题。

（2）在用户接入切换方面，无中心协调的海量并发切换会使个体接入容量、服务连续性与系统级长期公平相互冲突。围绕这一问题，本文设计基于局部公平性估计的分布式协作接入切换机制，即公平性引导的多智能体近端策略优化（Fairness-Guided Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization，FG-MAPPO）方法。该方法的创新点在于将广义 Gini 社会福利函数转化为用户可在邻域内估计和交换的优势修正量，并采用吞吐与公平分立的双目标学习结构，使各用户在仅依赖本地观测和有限邻域信令的条件下，兼顾候选卫星链路质量、负载状态和长期接入公平性；同时，所设计的协作训练与执行机制兼容 3GPP 条件切换（Conditional Handover，CHO）流程。仿真结果表明，在仅交互邻域信息的条件下，FG-MAPPO 的广义 Gini 公平性指标较具备全局信息的集中式匈牙利算法提升 $3.48\%$、平均速率提升 $6.91\%$；相较分布式强化学习基线，其切换成功率与负载均衡性能最高分别提升 $15.65\%$ 与 $30.91\%$，表明其能够在缺乏集中式协调的海量并发切换中同时维持接入效率、服务连续性和长期公平。

（3）在无线资源分配方面，多用户竞争与业务突发会使资源决策对用户级 QoS 的影响呈现滞后性和随机波动，反应式调度难以长期维持 QoS 可行性。围绕这一问题，本文设计基于生成式网络态势感知的切片--MAC 分层协作优化框架，即双时间尺度预测调度（Dual-Timescale Predictive Scheduling，DTPS）框架。该框架的创新点在于以带宽与功率预算为跨层接口、以 QoS 可行性为协同准则，将长期 QoS 保障分解为切片层的 QoS 可行域保障与 MAC 层的实时调度：切片层利用世界模型生成业务到达、队列演化和信道变化的未来轨迹，在线预测预算配置对速率--时延需求的支撑能力，并提前确定切片带宽与功率预算；MAC 层采用 QCI 优先级背包调度，估计候选业务流满足速率--时延门限所需的最小资源块组（Resource Block Group，RBG）和功率开销，将受限资源优先配置给 QoS 目标可满足的业务流。在 Starlink 规模星座仿真中，DTPS 在过载业务下实现 $80.8\%$ 的用户级 QoS 满足率，显著高于 TDRL-RSUA、RL-JRSS、LACO 与 DQN-MAC 等基线的 $33.0\%$--$56.3\%$，并将平均时延保持在 $78.3$~ms；突发不确定性增强时，其 QoS 满足率始终维持在 $97.6\%$ 以上，表明其能够在无线资源受限且业务快速波动的动态 LEO 星座中维持用户级 QoS 满足能力。

（4）在语义可靠传输方面，星上计算资源受限时，语义传输难以依靠扩展收发模型复杂度来改善任务性能，必须在任务精度、链路开销与星上计算开销之间建立新的可靠传输路径。围绕这一问题，本文设计基于任务反馈与协同增量重传的轻量化语义传输机制，即协同增量语义混合自动重传请求（Coordinated Incremental Semantic HARQ，CIS-HARQ）机制。该机制的创新点在于将传统完整重传重构为任务反馈引导的选择性增量编码与跨轮合并：任务感知接收端根据当前接收语义状态与任务判别特征之间的差距生成反馈，星上发射端依据反馈生成受码率约束的语义增量，接收端再通过掩码引导的门控语义合并逐轮恢复任务相关特征。其低开销实现进一步包括基于任务判决裕量归因的语义分块价值排序、基于熵估计的增量码率约束和发射端基于 SFT 的反馈条件化编码。在 ImageNet-100、CIFAR-100 与 VeRi-776 数据集、Rician 衰落信道、信噪比 $5$~dB 且信道带宽比（Channel Bandwidth Ratio，CBR）约 $0.02$ 的紧带宽条件下，CIS-HARQ 经三轮增量传输将下游任务精度由同带宽基线的 $35.7\%$ 提升至 $65.0\%$；其以约 $2.2$~M 参数与毫秒级星上推理时延，较生成式扩散接收端减少约 $2500$ 倍参数量，任务精度差距控制在约 $7\%$ 以内，表明轻量化增量重传能够在受限星上算力下逼近高复杂度语义模型的任务性能。

综上，本文围绕大规模 LEO 星座的端到端 QoS 保障问题，构建了覆盖可信感知、公平接入、高效资源分配和低开销可靠传输的分布式闭环管控体系。四项关键技术沿端到端业务承载路径形成清晰接口关系：多源置信度调控的分布式局部网络状态感知支撑分布式路由决策，基于局部公平性估计的分布式协作接入切换保障用户接入连续性，基于生成式网络态势感知的切片--MAC 分层协作优化提升用户级 QoS 满足能力，基于任务反馈与协同增量重传的轻量化语义传输在受限链路和星上算力条件下提升任务相关信息恢复能力。星座规模仿真和语义传输实验表明，所提方法能够在可靠全局状态缺失且地面实时控制闭环受限的条件下，有效提升无线网络态势感知可靠性、接入公平性、过载业务下的用户级 QoS 满足率以及紧带宽条件下的任务精度。本文将大规模 LEO 星座资源管控由分层独立优化推进至面向端到端 QoS 的分布式协同管控，并在有限邻域信令、可控星上计算预算和兼容 NR-NTN/CHO 流程的约束下，为 6G 空天地一体化网络中的星上边缘自治提供了可验证的技术路径。
\end{abstract}

\begin{abstract}[en]
Within the IMT-2030 vision of sixth-generation (6G) mobile communications, low Earth orbit (LEO) satellite networks are moving beyond complementary coverage systems and becoming a key component of the 6G infrastructure for non-terrestrial access, inter-satellite backhaul, and globally continuous service provisioning. As regenerative payloads, inter-satellite links, and onboard processing capability mature, network management in LEO constellations is moving from ground-centric centralized configuration toward onboard autonomous decision-making and neighborhood-level coordination. However, large-scale LEO constellations are characterized by fast topology variation, incomplete telemetry, constrained backhaul, and tight onboard computing and storage budgets. These features make it difficult to directly inherit the terrestrial cellular paradigm that relies on global-state collection and centralized scheduling. To serve as a core bearer network in 6G space-air-ground integrated systems, LEO constellations need distributed resource-management mechanisms that provide end-to-end quality-of-service (QoS) assurance along the full service path.

End-to-end QoS assurance is not determined by a single link or a single protocol layer. It depends on the joint behavior of network-state awareness, user access, radio-resource allocation, and service transmission. From the service-path perspective, end-to-end QoS assurance can be decomposed into four subproblems: awareness, access, resource allocation, and transmission. From the system-capability perspective, it requires communication reliability, resource-allocation efficiency, and access fairness to be jointly maintained. Under onboard distributed management, these requirements lead to four major challenges: degraded telemetry weakens the trustworthiness of local multi-hop network views and affects routing and resource decisions; massive users perform concurrent handovers without central coordination, causing conflicts among individual access capacity, service continuity, and system-level access fairness; bursty traffic and time-varying channels make timely coordination between slice-level budgets and medium access control (MAC)-layer scheduling difficult, while scarce radio resources may be consumed by flows whose QoS targets cannot be satisfied; and for task-oriented services such as image sensing and recognition, limited onboard computing resources make it difficult to improve task performance simply by increasing transmitter-side encoding capability or receiver-side recovery capability. To address these issues, this dissertation proposes a distributed cooperative resource-management method for end-to-end QoS assurance in large-scale LEO constellations. It replaces global-state synchronization and centralized optimization with local state calibration, limited neighborhood interaction, and cross-node cooperation. Specifically, four key techniques are designed along the end-to-end service path: a multi-source confidence-regulated distributed local network-state awareness framework, a distributed cooperative access-handover mechanism based on local fairness estimation, a slice--MAC hierarchical cooperative optimization framework based on generative network situation awareness, and a lightweight semantic transmission mechanism based on task feedback and coordinated incremental retransmission. Together, these techniques form a closed-loop assurance framework from state acquisition and access establishment to resource allocation and service transmission.

(1) For network-state awareness, degraded telemetry undermines the trustworthiness of local multi-hop network views, leaving distributed routing without reliable state inputs. To address this problem, this dissertation designs the multi-source confidence-regulated distributed local network-state awareness framework, namely Confidence-Adaptive Local Distributed Processing (CA-LDP). Its innovation lies in a calibrate-before-aggregate mechanism for trustworthy state awareness. CA-LDP performs confidence-adaptive graph-signal restoration for stale and missing telemetry according to information freshness, neighborhood spatial correlation, and current observation quality. It further models multi-hop state propagation with a graph reaction--diffusion process and uses confidence-regulated graph-signal aggregation to prevent low-confidence nodes from contaminating reliable local views. Distributed routing can therefore obtain trustworthy local network views without global-state collection. In a Starlink constellation with 1584 satellites and nine controlled telemetry-degradation scenarios, CA-LDP improves the average transmission rate by $4.8\%$--$10.8\%$, reduces the end-to-end delay by $6.7\%$--$11.1\%$, and maintains a routing success ratio close to $100\%$ over representative baselines. These results show that CA-LDP addresses the lack of reliable state inputs for distributed routing under degraded telemetry.

(2) For user access handover, massive concurrent handovers without central coordination create a conflict among individual access capacity, service continuity, and system-level long-term fairness. To address this problem, this dissertation designs a distributed cooperative access-handover mechanism based on local fairness estimation, termed Fairness-Guided Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (FG-MAPPO). Its innovation lies in converting the generalized Gini social welfare objective into a locally estimated advantage correction exchanged within neighborhoods, and in using a dual-objective learning structure that separates throughput optimization from fairness coordination. Each user can therefore make handover decisions from local observations and limited neighborhood signaling while jointly considering candidate-link quality, satellite load, and long-term access fairness. The cooperative training and execution procedure is also compatible with the 3GPP Conditional Handover (CHO) process. With neighborhood information exchange only, FG-MAPPO improves the generalized Gini fairness metric by $3.48\%$ and the average rate by $6.91\%$ compared with a centralized Hungarian algorithm with global information. Compared with distributed reinforcement-learning baselines, it improves the handover success ratio and load-balancing performance by up to $15.65\%$ and $30.91\%$, respectively. These results show that FG-MAPPO maintains access efficiency, service continuity, and long-term fairness under decentralized massive handover.

(3) For radio-resource allocation, multi-user competition and bursty services make the QoS effect of resource decisions delayed and stochastic, so reactive scheduling cannot maintain long-term QoS feasibility. To address this problem, this dissertation designs a slice--MAC hierarchical cooperative optimization framework based on generative network situation awareness, namely Dual-Timescale Predictive Scheduling (DTPS). Its innovation lies in using bandwidth and power budgets as the cross-layer interface and QoS feasibility as the coordination criterion, decomposing long-term QoS assurance into slice-layer QoS feasible-region maintenance and MAC-layer real-time scheduling. At the slice layer, a world model generates future trajectories of traffic arrivals, queue evolution, and channel variation, predicts the support capability of budget configurations for rate--delay requirements, and determines slice bandwidth and power budgets in advance. At the MAC layer, QCI-priority knapsack scheduling estimates the minimum resource block group (RBG) and power cost required for each candidate flow to satisfy its rate--delay threshold, assigning scarce resources preferentially to flows whose QoS targets can be satisfied. In Starlink-scale simulations, DTPS achieves a user-level QoS satisfaction ratio of $80.8\%$ under overloaded traffic, substantially outperforming TDRL-RSUA, RL-JRSS, LACO, and DQN-MAC baselines with satisfaction ratios of $33.0\%$--$56.3\%$, while keeping the average delay at $78.3$~ms. When burst uncertainty increases, its QoS satisfaction ratio remains above $97.6\%$. These results show that DTPS maintains user-level QoS satisfaction in dynamic LEO constellations with constrained radio resources and rapidly varying service demand.

(4) For reliable semantic transmission, limited onboard computing resources make it difficult to improve semantic task performance by expanding transceiver model complexity, requiring a transmission path that balances task accuracy, link overhead, and onboard computing cost. To address this problem, this dissertation designs a lightweight semantic transmission mechanism based on task feedback and coordinated incremental retransmission, namely Coordinated Incremental Semantic HARQ (CIS-HARQ). Its innovation lies in reformulating conventional full retransmission as task-feedback-guided selective incremental encoding and cross-round combining. The task-aware receiver generates feedback according to the gap between the current received semantic state and the task-discriminative features, the onboard transmitter generates rate-constrained semantic increments conditioned on this feedback, and the receiver progressively recovers task-relevant features through mask-guided gated semantic combining. Its low-overhead implementation further includes task-decision-margin attribution for semantic-block value ranking, entropy-based incremental-rate constraints, feedback-conditioned encoding through SFT at the transmitter, and gated semantic merging at the receiver. On the ImageNet-100, CIFAR-100, and VeRi-776 datasets over a Rician fading channel, at an SNR of $5$~dB and a tight channel bandwidth ratio (CBR) of about $0.02$, CIS-HARQ improves downstream task accuracy from $35.7\%$ for the equal-bandwidth baseline to $65.0\%$ after three incremental transmission rounds. With about $2.2$~M onboard parameters and millisecond-level inference latency, it reduces the parameter count by a factor of about $2500$ compared with a generative diffusion receiver, while keeping the task-accuracy gap within about $7\%$. These results show that lightweight incremental retransmission can approach the task performance of high-complexity semantic models under limited onboard computing resources.

In summary, this dissertation addresses end-to-end QoS assurance in large-scale LEO constellations and builds a distributed closed-loop management framework covering trustworthy network-state awareness, fair access, efficient resource allocation, and low-overhead reliable transmission. The four proposed techniques are connected along the end-to-end service path through explicit interfaces: multi-source confidence-regulated distributed local network-state awareness supports distributed routing decisions; distributed cooperative access handover based on local fairness estimation maintains user access continuity; slice--MAC hierarchical cooperative optimization based on generative network situation awareness improves user-level QoS satisfaction capability; and lightweight semantic transmission based on task feedback and coordinated incremental retransmission improves task-relevant information recovery under constrained links and onboard computing budgets. Constellation-scale simulations and semantic-transmission experiments show that the proposed methods effectively improve the reliability of network-state awareness, access fairness, user-level QoS satisfaction under overloaded traffic, and task accuracy under tight bandwidth when reliable global states are unavailable and real-time ground control loops are limited. By explicitly accounting for limited neighborhood signaling, bounded onboard computing budgets, and protocol interfaces compatible with NR-NTN/CHO procedures, this dissertation advances resource management for large-scale LEO constellations from isolated layer-wise optimization to end-to-end QoS-oriented distributed cooperative management, and provides a verifiable technical path toward onboard edge autonomy in 6G space-air-ground integrated networks.
\end{abstract}
