\begin{abstract}[zh]
在 IMT-2030 所描绘的第六代移动通信（6G）体系中，低轨（Low Earth Orbit，LEO）卫星网络正由补充覆盖系统演进为支撑非地面接入、星间回传与全球连续业务承载的关键基础设施。随着再生式载荷、星间链路和星上处理能力逐步成熟，LEO 星座的网络管控由地面集中配置转向星上自治决策和邻域协同。然而，大规模 LEO 星座具有拓扑高速变化、遥测状态不完备、回传链路受限、星上计算与存储资源受限等特征，难以直接沿用地面蜂窝网络依赖全局状态汇聚和中心化调度的管控范式。面向 6G 空天地一体化网络，LEO 星座需要从透明转发型覆盖系统进一步演进为具备端到端服务质量（Quality of Service，QoS）保障能力的核心承载网。

端到端 QoS 保障并非由单一链路或单一协议层独立决定，而是依赖网络状态感知、用户接入、无线资源分配与业务传输等环节的协同。在星上分布式管控条件下，上述过程面临四类关键挑战：其一，陈旧、缺失和不可靠遥测会削弱本地多跳网络视图的可信度，影响路由与资源决策的可靠性；其二，海量用户在无中心协调条件下并发切换，导致个体接入速率、服务连续性与系统级接入公平性之间相互制约；其三，业务突发和信道波动使切片层预算与媒体接入控制（Medium Access Control，MAC）层调度难以及时协同，有限无线资源容易被分配给 QoS 无法满足的业务流；其四，面向图像感知与识别等任务型业务，星上发送端难以部署复杂任务模型，受限链路下的任务相关信息交付需要同时满足传输可靠性、任务精度与星上开销约束。针对上述问题，本文以本地状态校准、邻域协同与跨层资源协作为基础，构建面向端到端 QoS 保障的分布式协同资源管控方法。该方法将全链路业务承载过程分解为可信状态感知、公平协作切换、切片--MAC 跨层资源分配和低开销语义可靠传输四项关键技术：可信状态感知为分布式路由提供可靠网络视图，公平协作切换保障接入连续性与长期公平，跨层资源分配实现用户级 QoS 保证，语义混合自动重传请求（Hybrid Automatic Repeat Request，HARQ）提升资源受限下的可靠传输能力，由此形成从状态获取、接入建立、资源配置到业务交付的闭环端到端保障体系。本文的主要工作和创新点如下：

（1）面向退化遥测条件下的分布式路由，本文提出置信度自适应分布式处理（Confidence-Adaptive Local Distributed Processing，CA-LDP）框架，以提升本地多跳网络状态感知的可靠性。该框架首先根据遥测信息的新鲜度、邻域空间相关性和当前观测质量，对陈旧与缺失状态进行置信度校准；随后在多跳状态传播过程中引入置信度约束，使高可信状态在邻域聚合中得到有效保留，并抑制低可信状态对可靠节点视图的污染。CA-LDP 使分布式路由能够在不依赖全局状态汇聚的情况下获得可信的局部网络视图。在由 1584 颗卫星组成的 Starlink 星座和九类受控遥测退化场景下，CA-LDP 相较代表性基线将平均传输速率提升 $4.8\%$--$10.8\%$，将端到端时延降低 $6.7\%$--$11.1\%$，路由成功率保持在接近 $100\%$ 的水平，验证了其在退化遥测条件下对分布式路由可靠性的增强作用。

（2）面向大规模用户并发切换，本文提出公平性引导的分布式协作切换方法（Fairness-Guided Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization，FG-MAPPO），以协调个体接入效率和系统级长期公平性。该方法将系统级接入公平目标转化为用户可在邻域内估计和交换的低开销公平性指示信息，使各用户在仅依赖本地观测和有限邻域信令的条件下，兼顾候选卫星链路质量、负载状态和长期接入公平性。为提升分布式切换的可部署性，本文进一步设计与 3GPP 条件切换（Conditional Handover，CHO）流程兼容的协作训练与执行机制，使切换决策能够在无中心协调条件下实现公平感知的接入选择。仿真结果表明，在仅交换有限邻域信息的条件下，FG-MAPPO 的广义 Gini 接入公平性指标较具备全局信息的集中式基线提升 $3.48\%$，平均速率提升 $6.91\%$；相较分布式切换基线，其切换成功率和负载均衡性能最高分别提升 $15.65\%$ 和 $30.91\%$，表明邻域公平性估计能够有效缓解海量并发切换中的局部拥塞与长期接入不均衡问题。

（3）面向多业务接入网无线资源分配，本文提出双时间尺度预测调度（Dual-Timescale Predictive Scheduling，DTPS）框架以实现和业务分组和无线资源映射的跨层调度。该框架以带宽和功率预算作为跨层控制量，基于世界模型在长时间尺度上预测不同预算配置对用户级速率--时延需求的满足能力，提前为 QoS 可达业务保留无线资源；在短时间尺度上，MAC 层根据实时信道、队列和业务优先级执行可行流调度。具体而言，切片层通过预测式资源预留维持用户级 QoS 可行域，MAC 层则估计候选业务流满足速率--时延门限所需的最小资源块组（Resource Block Group，RBG）和功率开销，优先调度满足 QCI/5QI 优先级且 QoS 可达的业务流。该设计避免了单纯基于瞬时队列与信道状态的调度中资源被不可达业务浪费的问题，使有限无线资源优先服务于能够获得 QoS 收益的用户。在 Starlink 规模星座仿真中，DTPS 在过载业务下实现 $80.8\%$ 的用户级 QoS 满足率，显著高于代表性基线的 $33.0\%$--$56.3\%$，平均时延保持在 $78.3$~ms；在单独考察业务突发不确定性的非过载场景中，其 QoS 满足率始终高于 $97.6\%$，验证了预测式切片预算和可达性感知 MAC 调度对长期用户级 QoS 的保障能力。

（4）面向受限链路上的任务相关信息交付，本文提出协同增量语义 HARQ（Coordinated Incremental Semantic HARQ，CIS-HARQ）机制，以协调端到端任务性能和星上发送端开销。针对星上载荷难以部署异构任务专用编解码器的问题，CIS-HARQ 将传统完整重传转化为反馈辅助的选择性语义精化：地面任务感知接收端在受限反馈预算下识别对任务判决最关键的语义分量，并通过低速率控制信令反馈给星上发送端；星上发送端仅保留轻量化增量编码器，依据反馈信息生成受码率约束的语义增量；接收端对多轮接收结果进行软合并和语义累积，从而在少量重传轮次内逐步恢复任务相关信息。该机制以有限反馈和增量传输替代星上复杂模型扩展，使语义通信能够更自然地嵌入端到端 QoS 保障链路。在 ImageNet-100、CIFAR-100 和 VeRi-776 数据集、Rician 衰落信道、信噪比 $5$~dB 且信道带宽比（Channel Bandwidth Ratio，CBR）约为 $0.02$ 的紧带宽条件下，CIS-HARQ 经三轮增量传输将下游任务精度由同带宽基线的 $35.7\%$ 提升至 $65.0\%$；其星上模型参数量约为 $2.2$~M，推理时延为毫秒级，参数量约为高复杂度生成式方案的 $1/2500$，并将任务精度差距控制在约 $7$ 个百分点以内。

综上，本文围绕大规模 LEO 星座的端到端 QoS 保障问题，构建了覆盖可信感知、公平接入、高效资源分配和低开销可靠传输的分布式闭环管控体系。四项关键技术沿端到端业务承载路径形成清晰接口关系：可信状态感知支撑分布式路由决策，公平协作切换保障用户接入连续性，切片--MAC 跨层资源分配提升用户级 QoS 可达性，协同增量语义 HARQ 降低受限链路上的任务相关信息交付开销。星座规模仿真和语义传输实验表明，所提方法能够在可靠全局状态缺失且地面实时控制闭环受限的条件下，有效提升网络状态感知可靠性、接入公平性、过载业务下的用户级 QoS 满足率以及紧带宽条件下的任务精度。本文将大规模 LEO 星座资源管控由分层独立优化推进至面向端到端 QoS 的分布式协同管控，并在有限邻域信令、可控星上计算预算和兼容 NR-NTN/CHO 流程的约束下，为 6G 空天地一体化网络中的星上边缘自治提供了可验证的技术路径。
\end{abstract}

\begin{abstract}[en]
In the IMT-2030 vision of sixth-generation (6G) mobile communications, low Earth orbit (LEO) satellite networks are evolving from complementary coverage systems into a key component of the 6G infrastructure for non-terrestrial access, inter-satellite backhaul, and globally continuous service provisioning. With the development of regenerative payloads, inter-satellite links, and onboard processing capability, network management in LEO constellations is moving from ground-centric centralized configuration toward onboard autonomous decision-making and neighborhood-level coordination. However, large-scale LEO constellations are characterized by fast topology variation, incomplete telemetry, constrained backhaul, and limited onboard computing and storage resources. These features make it difficult to directly inherit the terrestrial cellular paradigm that relies on global-state collection and centralized scheduling. To serve as a core bearer network in 6G space-air-ground integrated systems, LEO constellations need distributed resource-management mechanisms that provide end-to-end quality-of-service (QoS) assurance along the whole service path.

End-to-end QoS assurance is not determined by a single link or a single protocol layer. It depends on the coordination of network-state awareness, user access, radio-resource allocation, and service delivery. Under onboard distributed management, four major challenges arise. First, stale, missing, and unreliable telemetry weakens the trustworthiness of local multi-hop network views, thereby affecting routing and resource decisions. Second, massive users may perform concurrent handovers without central coordination, causing conflicts among individual access rate, service continuity, and system-level access fairness. Third, bursty traffic and time-varying channels make timely coordination between slice-level budgets and medium access control (MAC)-layer scheduling difficult, and scarce radio resources may be consumed by flows whose QoS targets are unattainable. Fourth, for task-oriented services such as image sensing and recognition, complex task-specific models are difficult to deploy at the onboard transmitter, and task-relevant information delivery over constrained links has to jointly meet transmission-reliability, task-accuracy, and onboard-overhead requirements. To address these issues, this dissertation develops distributed cooperative resource-management methods for end-to-end QoS assurance, based on local state calibration, limited neighborhood signaling, neighborhood cooperation, and cross-layer resource interfaces. The proposed framework decomposes the end-to-end service path into four key techniques: trustworthy network-state awareness, fair cooperative handover, slice--MAC cross-layer resource allocation, and low-overhead reliable semantic transmission. Trustworthy network-state awareness supports distributed routing with reliable network views; fair cooperative handover maintains access continuity and long-term fairness; cross-layer resource allocation improves user-level QoS attainability; and semantic hybrid automatic repeat request (HARQ) reduces the overhead of task-relevant information delivery over constrained links. Together, these techniques form a closed-loop assurance framework from state acquisition and access establishment to resource configuration and service delivery. The main contributions are summarized as follows.

First, for distributed routing under degraded telemetry, this dissertation proposes a Confidence-Adaptive Local Distributed Processing (CA-LDP) framework to improve the reliability of local multi-hop network-state awareness. CA-LDP first calibrates stale and missing telemetry according to information freshness, neighborhood spatial correlation, and the quality of the latest observation. It then introduces confidence-constrained multi-hop state propagation, so that high-confidence states are preserved during neighborhood aggregation while low-confidence states are prevented from contaminating reliable local views. Unlike methods that separate telemetry recovery from multi-hop aggregation, CA-LDP carries confidence information through both telemetry calibration and state propagation, enabling distributed routing to obtain trustworthy local network views without global-state collection. In a Starlink constellation with 1584 satellites and nine controlled telemetry-degradation scenarios, CA-LDP improves the average transmission rate by $4.8\%$--$10.8\%$, reduces the end-to-end delay by $6.7\%$--$11.1\%$, and maintains a routing success ratio close to $100\%$ over representative baselines. These results verify its capability to enhance distributed routing reliability under degraded telemetry.

Second, for massive concurrent handovers, this dissertation proposes a distributed cooperative handover method, termed Fairness-Guided Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (FG-MAPPO), to coordinate individual access efficiency and system-level long-term fairness. The proposed method converts the system-level access-fairness objective into compact fairness indicators that can be estimated and exchanged within a local neighborhood. Each user therefore makes handover decisions based on local observations and limited neighborhood signaling, while jointly considering candidate-link quality, satellite load, and long-term access fairness. To improve deployability, a cooperative training and execution procedure compatible with the 3GPP Conditional Handover (CHO) process is further designed, enabling fairness-aware access selection without central coordination. Simulation results show that, with limited neighborhood information exchange only, FG-MAPPO improves the generalized Gini access-fairness metric by $3.48\%$ and the average rate by $6.91\%$ compared with a centralized baseline using global information. Compared with distributed handover baselines, it improves the handover success ratio and load-balancing performance by up to $15.65\%$ and $30.91\%$, respectively. These results demonstrate that neighborhood-level fairness estimation can effectively mitigate local congestion and long-term access imbalance in massive handover scenarios.

Third, for multi-service radio-resource allocation in the access network, this dissertation proposes a Dual-Timescale Predictive Scheduling (DTPS) framework, which establishes a QoS-attainability interface between slice-level budgets and MAC-layer scheduling. Bandwidth and power budgets are used as cross-layer control variables. On a relatively long time scale, the slice layer predicts the capability of different budget configurations to support user-level rate--delay requirements and reserves radio resources for QoS-attainable services in advance. On a short time scale, the MAC layer performs feasible-flow scheduling according to instantaneous channel states, queue states, and service priorities. Specifically, the slice layer maintains a user-level QoS feasible region through predictive resource reservation, while the MAC layer estimates the minimum resource block group (RBG) and power cost required for each candidate flow to satisfy its rate--delay threshold. Flows are then scheduled according to their QCI/5QI priorities only when their QoS targets are attainable. This design prevents scarce radio resources from being consumed by unattainable flows and directs them to users that can obtain effective QoS gains. In Starlink-scale simulations, DTPS achieves a user-level QoS satisfaction ratio of $80.8\%$ under overloaded traffic, substantially outperforming representative baselines with satisfaction ratios of $33.0\%$--$56.3\%$, while keeping the average delay at $78.3$~ms. In separate non-overloaded scenarios for traffic-burst uncertainty evaluation, its QoS satisfaction ratio remains above $97.6\%$, confirming the effectiveness of predictive slice budgeting and attainability-aware MAC scheduling for long-term user-level QoS assurance.

Fourth, for task-relevant information delivery over constrained links, this dissertation proposes a Coordinated Incremental Semantic HARQ (CIS-HARQ) mechanism to balance end-to-end task performance and onboard transmitter overhead. Since it is impractical to deploy heterogeneous task-specific codecs at the satellite payload, CIS-HARQ transforms conventional full retransmission into feedback-assisted selective semantic refinement. Under a limited feedback budget, the task-aware ground receiver identifies the semantic components that are most critical to task decisions and feeds them back to the onboard transmitter through a low-rate control channel. The onboard transmitter keeps only a lightweight incremental encoder and generates rate-constrained semantic increments according to the received feedback. The receiver then performs soft combining and semantic accumulation over multiple HARQ rounds, progressively recovering task-relevant information with a small number of retransmissions. In this way, limited feedback and incremental transmission replace the expansion of onboard model complexity, allowing semantic communication to be integrated into the end-to-end QoS assurance chain. On the ImageNet-100, CIFAR-100, and VeRi-776 datasets over a Rician fading channel, at an SNR of $5$~dB and a tight channel bandwidth ratio (CBR) of about $0.02$, CIS-HARQ improves downstream task accuracy from $35.7\%$ for the equal-bandwidth baseline to $65.0\%$ after three incremental transmission rounds. With about $2.2$~M onboard parameters and millisecond-level inference latency, it reduces the parameter count by a factor of about $2500$ compared with a high-complexity generative scheme, while keeping the task-accuracy gap within about $7$ percentage points.

In summary, this dissertation addresses end-to-end QoS assurance in large-scale LEO constellations and builds a distributed closed-loop management framework covering trustworthy network-state awareness, fair access, efficient resource allocation, and low-overhead reliable transmission. The four proposed techniques are connected along the end-to-end service path through explicit interfaces: trustworthy network-state awareness supports distributed routing decisions, fair cooperative handover maintains user access continuity, slice--MAC cross-layer resource allocation improves user-level QoS attainability, and coordinated incremental semantic HARQ reduces the overhead of task-relevant information delivery over constrained links. Constellation-scale simulations and semantic-transmission experiments show that the proposed methods effectively improve the reliability of network-state awareness, access fairness, user-level QoS satisfaction under overloaded traffic, and task accuracy under tight bandwidth when reliable global states are unavailable and ground-based real-time control loops are constrained. By explicitly accounting for limited neighborhood signaling, bounded onboard computing budgets, and protocol interfaces compatible with NR-NTN/CHO procedures, this dissertation advances resource management for large-scale LEO constellations from isolated layer-wise optimization to end-to-end QoS-oriented distributed cooperative management, and provides a verifiable technical path toward onboard edge autonomy in 6G space-air-ground integrated networks.
\end{abstract}
