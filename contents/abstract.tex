\begin{abstract}[zh]
在 IMT-2030 描绘的第六代移动通信（6G）体系中，低轨（Low Earth Orbit，LEO）卫星网络正由补充覆盖系统演进为支撑非地面接入、星间回传与全球连续业务承载的关键基础设施。随着再生式载荷、星间链路和星上处理能力逐步成熟，LEO 星座的网络管控模式正由地面集中配置转向星上自治决策与邻域协同。然而，大规模 LEO 星座具有拓扑高速变化、遥测信息不完备、回传链路受限以及星上计算与存储资源有限等特征，难以直接沿用地面蜂窝网络中依赖全局状态汇聚和集中式调度的管控范式。面向 6G 空天地一体化网络，LEO 星座需要从透明转发型覆盖系统进一步演进为具备端到端服务质量（Quality of Service，QoS）保障能力的核心承载网络。

端到端 QoS 保障并非由单一链路或单一协议层独立决定，而是依赖无线网络态势感知、用户接入、无线资源分配与业务传输等环节的协同。在星上分布式管控条件下，上述过程面临四类关键挑战：其一，陈旧、缺失和不可靠遥测会削弱本地多跳网络视图的可信度，影响路由与资源决策的可靠性；其二，海量用户在无中心协调条件下并发切换，导致个体接入速率、服务连续性与系统级接入公平性之间相互制约；其三，业务突发和信道波动使切片层预算与媒体接入控制（Medium Access Control，MAC）层调度难以及时协同，有限无线资源容易被分配给 QoS 目标难以满足的业务流；其四，面向图像感知与识别等任务型业务，星上计算资源受限使语义传输难以简单依靠提升发射端编码模型的表达能力，或增强接收端恢复模型的纠错与恢复能力来改善任务性能，受限链路下的任务相关信息传输需要同时满足传输可靠性、任务精度与星上开销约束。针对上述问题，本文以本地状态校准、有限邻域信令、邻域协同与跨层资源接口为基础，构建面向端到端 QoS 保障的分布式协同资源管控方法。该方法将全链路业务承载过程分解为多源置信度调控的分布式局部网络状态感知、基于局部公平性估计的分布式协作接入切换、基于生成式网络态势感知的切片--MAC 分层协作优化和基于任务反馈与协同增量重传的轻量化语义传输四项关键技术：多源置信度调控为分布式路由提供可靠网络视图，局部公平性估计保障接入连续性与长期公平，生成式网络态势感知支撑切片--MAC 分层协同并提升用户级 QoS 满足能力，任务反馈与协同增量重传在受限链路和星上算力条件下提升任务相关信息恢复能力，由此形成从状态获取、接入建立、资源配置到业务承载的闭环端到端保障体系。本文的主要工作和创新点如下。

（1）面向退化遥测条件下的分布式路由，本文提出多源置信度调控的分布式局部网络状态感知框架，即置信度自适应分布式处理（Confidence-Adaptive Local Distributed Processing，CA-LDP）框架，以提升本地多跳网络状态感知的可靠性。该框架首先根据遥测信息的新鲜度、邻域空间相关性和当前观测质量，对陈旧与缺失状态进行置信度校准；随后以图反应--扩散模型刻画多跳状态传播，并在传播过程中引入置信度约束，使高可信状态在邻域聚合中得到有效保留，并抑制低可信状态对可靠节点视图的污染。与将状态恢复和多跳聚合分离处理的方法不同，CA-LDP 将置信度贯穿于遥测校准和状态传播全过程，使分布式路由能够在不依赖全局状态汇聚的情况下获得可信的局部网络视图。在由 1584 颗卫星组成的 Starlink 星座和九类受控遥测退化场景下，CA-LDP 相较代表性基线将平均传输速率提升 $4.8\%$--$10.8\%$，将端到端时延降低 $6.7\%$--$11.1\%$，路由成功率保持在接近 $100\%$ 的水平。上述结果表明，CA-LDP 能够在陈旧、缺失和不可靠遥测并存时维持可信的本地多跳网络视图，解决退化遥测条件下分布式路由缺少可靠状态输入的问题。

（2）面向大规模用户并发切换，本文提出基于局部公平性估计的分布式协作接入切换机制，即公平性引导的分布式协作切换方法（Fairness-Guided Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization，FG-MAPPO），以协调个体接入效率和系统级长期公平性。该方法将系统级接入公平目标转化为用户可在邻域内估计和交换的低开销公平性指示信息，使各用户在仅依赖本地观测和有限邻域信令的条件下，兼顾候选卫星链路质量、负载状态和长期接入公平性。为提升分布式切换的可部署性，本文进一步设计与 3GPP 条件切换（Conditional Handover，CHO）流程兼容的协作训练与执行机制，使切换决策能够在无中心协调条件下实现公平感知的接入选择。仿真结果表明，在仅交换有限邻域信息的条件下，FG-MAPPO 的广义 Gini 接入公平性指标较具备全局信息的集中式基线提升 $3.48\%$，平均速率提升 $6.91\%$；相较分布式切换基线，其切换成功率和负载均衡性能最高分别提升 $15.65\%$ 和 $30.91\%$。上述结果表明，FG-MAPPO 能够在无中心协调的海量并发切换中同时维持接入效率、服务连续性和长期公平，解决动态覆盖下个体接入收益与系统级接入公平难以兼顾的问题。

（3）面向多业务接入网无线资源分配，本文提出基于生成式网络态势感知的切片--MAC 分层协作优化框架，即双时间尺度预测调度（Dual-Timescale Predictive Scheduling，DTPS）框架，在切片预算和 MAC 层调度之间建立面向 QoS 满足的跨层接口。该框架以带宽和功率预算作为跨层控制量，在较长时间尺度上基于世界模型生成预算条件下的用户级 QoS 演化轨迹，预测不同预算配置对速率--时延需求的支撑能力，并提前为 QoS 目标可满足的业务保留无线资源；在较短时间尺度上，MAC 层根据实时信道、队列和业务优先级执行可行流调度。具体而言，切片层通过预测式资源预留维持用户级 QoS 可行域，MAC 层则估计候选业务流满足速率--时延门限所需的最小资源块组（Resource Block Group，RBG）和功率开销，优先调度满足 QCI/5QI 优先级且 QoS 目标可满足的业务流。该设计避免了单纯基于瞬时队列与信道状态的调度中资源被难以满足目标的业务流消耗，使有限无线资源优先服务于能够获得 QoS 收益的用户。在 Starlink 规模星座仿真中，DTPS 在过载业务下实现 $80.8\%$ 的用户级 QoS 满足率，显著高于 TDRL-RSUA、RL-JRSS、LACO 与 DQN-MAC 等基线的 $33.0\%$--$56.3\%$，平均时延保持在 $78.3$~ms；在单独考察业务突发不确定性的非过载场景中，其 QoS 满足率始终高于 $97.6\%$。上述结果表明，DTPS 能够在无线资源受限且业务快速波动的动态 LEO 星座中维持用户级 QoS 满足能力，解决用户级 QoS 难以长期保障的问题。

（4）面向受限链路上的任务相关信息传输，本文提出基于任务反馈与协同增量重传的轻量化语义传输机制，即协同增量语义 HARQ（Coordinated Incremental Semantic HARQ，CIS-HARQ）机制，以协调端到端任务性能、链路开销与星上计算开销。针对星上计算资源受限条件下难以通过增强发射端编码表达能力或接收端纠错恢复能力改善任务性能的问题，CIS-HARQ 将传统完整重传转化为任务反馈引导的选择性增量编码与跨轮合并：地面任务感知接收端在受限反馈预算下，通过任务判决裕量归因评估语义传输块价值，识别对任务判决最关键的语义分量，并通过低速率控制信令反馈给星上发射端；星上发射端依据反馈信息生成受码率约束的语义增量；接收端对多轮接收结果进行门控合并和语义累积，从而在少量重传轮次内逐步恢复任务相关信息。该机制以有限反馈和增量传输替代高复杂度收发模型扩展，使语义通信能够更自然地嵌入端到端 QoS 保障链路。在 ImageNet-100、CIFAR-100 和 VeRi-776 数据集、Rician 衰落信道、信噪比 $5$~dB 且信道带宽比（Channel Bandwidth Ratio，CBR）约为 $0.02$ 的紧带宽条件下，CIS-HARQ 经三轮增量传输将下游任务精度由同带宽基线的 $35.7\%$ 提升至 $65.0\%$；其星上模型参数量约为 $2.2$~M，推理时延为毫秒级，参数量约为高复杂度生成式方案的 $1/2500$，并将任务精度差距控制在约 $7$ 个百分点以内。上述结果表明，CIS-HARQ 能够在紧带宽和受限星上计算资源下恢复任务相关特征，解决任务性能、传输可靠性和星上开销难以同时满足的问题。

综上，本文围绕大规模 LEO 星座的端到端 QoS 保障问题，构建了覆盖可信感知、公平接入、高效资源分配和低开销可靠传输的分布式闭环管控体系。四项关键技术沿端到端业务承载路径形成清晰接口关系：多源置信度调控的分布式局部网络状态感知支撑分布式路由决策，基于局部公平性估计的分布式协作接入切换保障用户接入连续性，基于生成式网络态势感知的切片--MAC 分层协作优化提升用户级 QoS 满足能力，基于任务反馈与协同增量重传的轻量化语义传输在受限链路和星上算力条件下提升任务相关信息恢复能力。星座规模仿真和语义传输实验表明，所提方法能够在可靠全局状态缺失且地面实时控制闭环受限的条件下，有效提升无线网络态势感知可靠性、接入公平性、过载业务下的用户级 QoS 满足率以及紧带宽条件下的任务精度。本文将大规模 LEO 星座资源管控由分层独立优化推进至面向端到端 QoS 的分布式协同管控，并在有限邻域信令、可控星上计算预算和兼容 NR-NTN/CHO 流程的约束下，为 6G 空天地一体化网络中的星上边缘自治提供了可验证的技术路径。
\end{abstract}

\begin{abstract}[en]
Within the IMT-2030 vision of sixth-generation (6G) mobile communications, low Earth orbit (LEO) satellite networks are moving beyond complementary coverage systems and becoming a key component of the 6G infrastructure for non-terrestrial access, inter-satellite backhaul, and globally continuous service provisioning. As regenerative payloads, inter-satellite links, and onboard processing capability mature, network management in LEO constellations is moving from ground-centric centralized configuration toward onboard autonomous decision-making and neighborhood-level coordination. However, large-scale LEO constellations are characterized by fast topology variation, incomplete telemetry, constrained backhaul, and tight onboard computing and storage budgets. These features make it difficult to directly inherit the terrestrial cellular paradigm that relies on global-state collection and centralized scheduling. To serve as a core bearer network in 6G space-air-ground integrated systems, LEO constellations need distributed resource-management mechanisms that provide end-to-end quality-of-service (QoS) assurance along the full service path.

End-to-end QoS assurance is not determined by a single link or a single protocol layer. It depends on the joint behavior of network-state awareness, user access, radio-resource allocation, and service transmission. Under onboard distributed management, four major challenges arise. First, stale, missing, or unreliable telemetry weakens the trustworthiness of local multi-hop network views, thereby affecting routing and resource decisions. Second, massive users may perform concurrent handovers without central coordination, causing conflicts among individual access rate, service continuity, and system-level access fairness. Third, bursty traffic and time-varying channels make timely coordination between slice-level budgets and medium access control (MAC)-layer scheduling difficult, and scarce radio resources may be consumed by flows whose QoS targets cannot be satisfied. Fourth, for task-oriented services such as image sensing and recognition, limited onboard computing resources make it difficult to improve task performance simply by increasing the representation capability of the transmitter-side encoding model or the error-recovery capability of the receiver-side reconstruction model, while task-relevant information transmission over constrained links has to jointly meet transmission-reliability, task-accuracy, and onboard-overhead requirements. To address these issues, this dissertation develops distributed cooperative resource-management methods for end-to-end QoS assurance, based on local state calibration, limited neighborhood signaling, neighborhood cooperation, and cross-layer resource interfaces. The proposed framework decomposes the end-to-end service path into four key techniques: a multi-source confidence-regulated distributed local network-state awareness framework, a distributed cooperative access-handover mechanism based on local fairness estimation, a slice--MAC hierarchical cooperative optimization framework based on generative network situation awareness, and a lightweight semantic transmission mechanism based on task feedback and coordinated incremental retransmission. Multi-source confidence regulation supports distributed routing with reliable network views; local fairness estimation maintains access continuity and long-term fairness; generative network situation awareness supports slice--MAC hierarchical coordination and improves user-level QoS satisfaction capability; and task feedback with coordinated incremental retransmission improves task-relevant information recovery under constrained links and onboard computing budgets. Together, these techniques form a closed-loop assurance framework from state acquisition and access establishment to resource configuration and service provisioning. The main contributions are summarized as follows.

First, for distributed routing under degraded telemetry, this dissertation proposes a multi-source confidence-regulated distributed local network-state awareness framework, namely Confidence-Adaptive Local Distributed Processing (CA-LDP), to improve the reliability of local multi-hop network-state awareness. CA-LDP first calibrates stale and missing telemetry according to information freshness, neighborhood spatial correlation, and the quality of the latest observation. It then models multi-hop state propagation with a graph reaction--diffusion process and introduces confidence constraints into the propagation, so that high-confidence states are preserved during neighborhood aggregation while low-confidence states are prevented from contaminating reliable local views. Unlike methods that separate telemetry recovery from multi-hop aggregation, CA-LDP carries confidence information through both telemetry calibration and state propagation, enabling distributed routing to obtain trustworthy local network views without global-state collection. In a Starlink constellation with 1584 satellites and nine controlled telemetry-degradation scenarios, CA-LDP improves the average transmission rate by $4.8\%$--$10.8\%$, reduces the end-to-end delay by $6.7\%$--$11.1\%$, and maintains a routing success ratio close to $100\%$ over representative baselines. These results show that CA-LDP maintains trustworthy local multi-hop network views under stale, missing, and unreliable telemetry, addressing the lack of reliable state inputs for distributed routing under degraded telemetry.

Second, for massive concurrent handovers, this dissertation proposes a distributed cooperative access-handover mechanism based on local fairness estimation, termed Fairness-Guided Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (FG-MAPPO), to coordinate individual access efficiency and system-level long-term fairness. The proposed method converts the system-level access-fairness objective into compact fairness indicators that can be estimated and exchanged within a local neighborhood. Each user therefore makes handover decisions based on local observations and limited neighborhood signaling, while jointly considering candidate-link quality, satellite load, and long-term access fairness. To improve deployability, a cooperative training and execution procedure compatible with the 3GPP Conditional Handover (CHO) process is further designed, enabling fairness-aware access selection without central coordination. Simulation results show that, with limited neighborhood information exchange only, FG-MAPPO improves the generalized Gini access-fairness metric by $3.48\%$ and the average rate by $6.91\%$ compared with a centralized baseline using global information. Compared with distributed handover baselines, it improves the handover success ratio and load-balancing performance by up to $15.65\%$ and $30.91\%$, respectively. These results show that FG-MAPPO maintains access efficiency, service continuity, and long-term fairness in massive concurrent handovers without central coordination, addressing the conflict between individual access gain and system-level access fairness under dynamic satellite coverage.

Third, for multi-service radio-resource allocation in the access network, this dissertation proposes a slice--MAC hierarchical cooperative optimization framework based on generative network situation awareness, namely Dual-Timescale Predictive Scheduling (DTPS), which establishes a QoS-satisfaction interface between slice-level budgets and MAC-layer scheduling. Bandwidth and power budgets are used as cross-layer control variables. On a relatively long time scale, the slice layer uses a world model to generate budget-conditioned user-level QoS evolution trajectories, predicts the capability of different budget configurations to support rate--delay requirements, and reserves radio resources in advance for services whose QoS targets can be satisfied. On a short time scale, the MAC layer performs feasible-flow scheduling according to instantaneous channel states, queue states, and service priorities. Specifically, the slice layer maintains a user-level QoS feasible region through predictive resource reservation, while the MAC layer estimates the minimum resource block group (RBG) and power cost required for each candidate flow to satisfy its rate--delay threshold. Flows are then scheduled according to their QCI/5QI priorities only when their QoS targets can be satisfied. This design prevents scarce radio resources from being consumed by flows whose QoS targets cannot be satisfied and directs them to users that can obtain effective QoS gains. In Starlink-scale simulations, DTPS achieves a user-level QoS satisfaction ratio of $80.8\%$ under overloaded traffic, substantially outperforming TDRL-RSUA, RL-JRSS, LACO, and DQN-MAC baselines with satisfaction ratios of $33.0\%$--$56.3\%$, while keeping the average delay at $78.3$~ms. In separate non-overloaded scenarios for traffic-burst uncertainty evaluation, its QoS satisfaction ratio remains above $97.6\%$. These results show that DTPS maintains user-level QoS satisfaction in dynamic LEO constellations with constrained radio resources and rapidly varying service demand, addressing the difficulty of long-term user-level QoS assurance.

Fourth, for task-relevant information transmission over constrained links, this dissertation proposes a lightweight semantic transmission mechanism based on task feedback and coordinated incremental retransmission, namely Coordinated Incremental Semantic HARQ (CIS-HARQ), to balance end-to-end task performance, link overhead, and onboard computing cost. Under limited onboard computing resources, it is difficult to improve task performance simply by increasing the representation capability of the transmitter-side encoder or the error-recovery capability of the receiver-side reconstruction model. To address this problem, CIS-HARQ transforms conventional full retransmission into task-feedback-guided selective incremental encoding and cross-round combining. Under a limited feedback budget, the task-aware ground receiver uses attribution analysis of the task-decision margin to assess semantic transmission-block value, identifies the semantic components that are most critical to task decisions, and feeds them back to the onboard transmitter through a low-rate control channel. The onboard transmitter generates rate-constrained semantic increments according to the received feedback, while the receiver performs gated combining and semantic accumulation over multiple HARQ rounds to progressively recover task-relevant information. In this way, limited feedback and incremental transmission replace high-complexity transceiver-model expansion, allowing semantic communication to be integrated into the end-to-end QoS assurance chain. On the ImageNet-100, CIFAR-100, and VeRi-776 datasets over a Rician fading channel, at an SNR of $5$~dB and a tight channel bandwidth ratio (CBR) of about $0.02$, CIS-HARQ improves downstream task accuracy from $35.7\%$ for the equal-bandwidth baseline to $65.0\%$ after three incremental transmission rounds. With about $2.2$~M onboard parameters and millisecond-level inference latency, it reduces the parameter count by a factor of about $2500$ compared with a high-complexity generative scheme, while keeping the task-accuracy gap within about $7$ percentage points. These results show that CIS-HARQ recovers task-relevant features under tight bandwidth and limited onboard computing resources, addressing the difficulty of jointly satisfying task performance, transmission reliability, and onboard-overhead requirements.

In summary, this dissertation addresses end-to-end QoS assurance in large-scale LEO constellations and builds a distributed closed-loop management framework covering trustworthy network-state awareness, fair access, efficient resource allocation, and low-overhead reliable transmission. The four proposed techniques are connected along the end-to-end service path through explicit interfaces: multi-source confidence-regulated distributed local network-state awareness supports distributed routing decisions; distributed cooperative access handover based on local fairness estimation maintains user access continuity; slice--MAC hierarchical cooperative optimization based on generative network situation awareness improves user-level QoS satisfaction capability; and lightweight semantic transmission based on task feedback and coordinated incremental retransmission improves task-relevant information recovery under constrained links and onboard computing budgets. Constellation-scale simulations and semantic-transmission experiments show that the proposed methods effectively improve the reliability of network-state awareness, access fairness, user-level QoS satisfaction under overloaded traffic, and task accuracy under tight bandwidth when reliable global states are unavailable and real-time ground control loops are limited. By explicitly accounting for limited neighborhood signaling, bounded onboard computing budgets, and protocol interfaces compatible with NR-NTN/CHO procedures, this dissertation advances resource management for large-scale LEO constellations from isolated layer-wise optimization to end-to-end QoS-oriented distributed cooperative management, and provides a verifiable technical path toward onboard edge autonomy in 6G space-air-ground integrated networks.
\end{abstract}
