\begin{abstract}[zh]
在 IMT-2030 描绘的第六代移动通信（6G）体系中，低轨（Low Earth Orbit，LEO）卫星网络正由补充覆盖系统演进为支撑非地面接入、星间回传与全球连续业务承载的重要基础设施。随着再生式载荷、星间链路和星上处理能力逐步成熟，LEO 星座的网络管控模式也由地面集中配置转向星上自治决策与邻域协同。与地面蜂窝网络相比，大规模 LEO 星座具有拓扑高速变化、遥测信息不完备、回传链路受限以及星上计算与存储资源有限等特征，难以直接沿用依赖全局状态汇聚和集中式调度的管控范式。面向 6G 空天地一体化网络，LEO 星座需要从透明转发型覆盖系统进一步演进为具备端到端服务质量（Quality of Service，QoS）保障能力的核心承载网络。

端到端 QoS 保障并非由单一链路或单一协议层独立决定，而是取决于无线网络态势感知、用户接入、无线资源分配与业务传输之间的连续协同。从业务承载过程看，端到端 QoS 保障可分解为网络状态感知、用户接入切换、无线资源分配与可靠业务传输四个子问题；从系统能力指标看，其核心在于同时支撑通信可靠性、资源分配效率与接入公平性。在星上分布式管控条件下，退化遥测会削弱本地多跳网络视图的可信度，海量并发切换会加剧个体接入容量与系统级公平之间的矛盾，业务突发和信道波动会使切片预算与媒体接入控制（Medium Access Control，MAC）层调度难以及时协同，而星上计算资源受限又限制了高复杂度语义收发模型的使用。本文据此提出面向大规模 LEO 星座端到端 QoS 保障的分布式协同资源管控方法，以本地状态校准、有限邻域交互和跨节点协作替代全局状态同步与集中式求解。该方法沿端到端业务承载过程设计四项关键技术：多源置信度调控的分布式局部网络状态感知框架、基于局部公平性估计的分布式协作接入切换机制、基于生成式网络态势感知的切片--MAC 分层协作优化框架，以及基于任务反馈与协同增量重传的轻量化语义传输机制。

（1）在网络状态感知方面，分布式路由首先受限于本地状态输入的可信性。退化遥测不仅会造成节点状态缺失和陈旧，还会在多跳聚合过程中向邻域扩散，从而削弱路由决策的可靠性。本文提出多源置信度调控的分布式局部网络状态感知框架，即置信度自适应局部分布式处理（Confidence-Adaptive Local Distributed Processing，CA-LDP）框架。该框架将置信度引入遥测修复和状态传播的全过程：在状态恢复阶段，根据信息新鲜度、邻域空间相关性和当前观测质量对陈旧与缺失遥测进行置信度自适应图信号修复；在多跳聚合阶段，以图反应--扩散模型刻画状态传播，并通过受置信度调制的图信号聚合抑制低置信节点对可靠节点视图的污染。由此，分布式路由无需全局状态汇聚即可获得可信的局部网络视图。在由 1584 颗卫星构成的 Starlink 真实星座和九类受控遥测退化场景下，CA-LDP 相较代表性基线将平均传输速率提升 $4.8\%$--$10.8\%$，端到端时延降低 $6.7\%$--$11.1\%$，并维持接近 $100\%$ 的路由成功率。

（2）在用户接入切换方面，海量用户在无中心协调条件下并发选择接入卫星，局部速率收益与系统级长期公平容易相互偏离。本文提出基于局部公平性估计的分布式协作接入切换机制，即公平性引导的多智能体近端策略优化（Fairness-Guided Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization，FG-MAPPO）方法。该方法将广义 Gini 社会福利函数转化为用户可在邻域内估计和交换的优势修正量，并采用吞吐与公平分立的双目标学习结构，使切换策略在保留个体速率性能的同时感知长期公平约束。所设计的协作训练与执行机制兼容 3GPP 条件切换（Conditional Handover，CHO）流程，可在仅依赖本地观测和有限邻域信令的条件下联合考虑候选卫星链路质量、负载状态和长期接入公平性。仿真结果表明，在仅交互邻域信息的条件下，FG-MAPPO 的广义 Gini 公平性指标较具备全局信息的集中式匈牙利算法提升 $3.48\%$，平均速率提升 $6.91\%$；相较分布式强化学习基线，其切换成功率与负载均衡性能最高分别提升 $15.65\%$ 与 $30.91\%$。

（3）在无线资源分配方面，多用户竞争、业务突发和信道波动使资源决策对用户级 QoS 的影响具有时延累积和随机波动特征，单纯依赖瞬时队列与信道状态的反应式调度难以维持长期 QoS 可行性。本文提出基于生成式网络态势感知的切片--MAC 分层协作优化框架，即双时间尺度预测调度（Dual-Timescale Predictive Scheduling，DTPS）框架。该框架以带宽与功率预算为跨层接口，以 QoS 可行性为协同准则，将长期 QoS 保障分解为切片层的 QoS 可行域保障与 MAC 层的实时调度。切片层利用世界模型生成业务到达、队列演化和信道变化的未来轨迹，在线预测预算配置对速率--时延需求的支撑能力，并提前确定切片带宽与功率预算；MAC 层采用 QCI 优先级背包调度，估计候选业务流满足速率--时延门限所需的最小资源块组（Resource Block Group，RBG）和功率开销，将受限资源优先配置给 QoS 目标可满足的业务流。在 Starlink 规模星座仿真中，DTPS 在过载业务下实现 $80.8\%$ 的用户级 QoS 满足率，显著高于 TDRL-RSUA、RL-JRSS、LACO 与 DQN-MAC 等基线的 $33.0\%$--$56.3\%$，并将平均时延保持在 $78.3$~ms；突发不确定性增强时，其 QoS 满足率始终维持在 $97.6\%$ 以上。

（4）在语义可靠传输方面，星上计算资源受限使语义通信难以通过扩展收发模型复杂度来提升任务性能。本文提出基于任务反馈与协同增量重传的轻量化语义传输机制，即协同增量语义混合自动重传请求（Coordinated Incremental Semantic HARQ，CIS-HARQ）机制，将可靠性增益从模型复杂度扩展转向通信轮次与反馈协作。该机制将传统完整重传重构为任务反馈引导的选择性增量编码与跨轮合并：任务感知接收端根据当前接收语义状态与任务判别特征之间的差距生成反馈，星上发射端依据反馈生成受码率约束的语义增量，接收端再通过掩码引导的门控语义合并逐轮恢复任务相关特征。其低开销实现包括基于任务判决裕量归因的语义分块价值排序、基于熵估计的增量码率约束和发射端基于 SFT 的反馈条件化编码。在 ImageNet-100、CIFAR-100 与 VeRi-776 数据集、Rician 衰落信道、信噪比 $5$~dB 且信道带宽比（Channel Bandwidth Ratio，CBR）约 $0.02$ 的紧带宽条件下，CIS-HARQ 经三轮增量传输将下游任务精度由同带宽基线的 $35.7\%$ 提升至 $65.0\%$；其以约 $2.2$~M 参数与毫秒级星上推理时延，较生成式扩散接收端减少约 $2500$ 倍参数量，任务精度差距控制在约 $7\%$ 以内。

综上，本文构建了覆盖可信网络状态感知、公平用户接入、高效无线资源分配和低开销可靠语义传输的分布式闭环管控体系。四项关键技术沿端到端业务承载过程形成递进关系：可信局部状态视图支撑分布式路由，局部公平性估计约束接入切换，生成式网络态势感知提前刻画资源预算对用户级 QoS 的支撑能力，任务反馈与协同增量重传在受限链路和星上算力条件下恢复任务相关信息。星座规模仿真和语义传输实验表明，所提方法能够在可靠全局状态缺失且地面实时控制闭环受限的条件下，提升网络状态感知可靠性、接入公平性、过载业务下的用户级 QoS 满足率以及紧带宽条件下的任务精度。本文将大规模 LEO 星座资源管控由分层独立优化推进至面向端到端 QoS 的分布式协同管控，并在有限邻域信令、可控星上计算预算和兼容 NR-NTN/CHO 流程的约束下，为 6G 空天地一体化网络中的星上边缘自治提供了可验证的技术路径。
\end{abstract}

\begin{abstract}[en]
Within the IMT-2030 vision of sixth-generation (6G) mobile communications, low Earth orbit (LEO) satellite networks are moving beyond complementary coverage and becoming an important infrastructure for non-terrestrial access, inter-satellite backhaul, and globally continuous services. As regenerative payloads, inter-satellite links, and onboard processing mature, network management in LEO constellations is moving from ground-centric centralized configuration toward onboard autonomous decision-making and neighborhood-level coordination. Compared with terrestrial cellular networks, large-scale LEO constellations have rapidly varying topology, incomplete telemetry, constrained backhaul, and tight onboard computing and storage budgets. These features make it difficult to inherit a management paradigm based on global-state collection and centralized scheduling. To serve as a core bearer network in 6G space-air-ground integrated systems, LEO constellations need distributed resource-management mechanisms that provide end-to-end quality-of-service (QoS) assurance along the full service path.

End-to-end QoS assurance is not determined by a single link or a single protocol layer. It depends on continuous coordination among network situation awareness, user access, radio-resource allocation, and service transmission. Along the service path, end-to-end QoS assurance can be decomposed into four subproblems: network-state awareness, user access handover, radio-resource allocation, and reliable service transmission. In terms of system capability, it requires communication reliability, resource-allocation efficiency, and access fairness to be jointly maintained. Under onboard distributed management, degraded telemetry weakens the trustworthiness of local multi-hop network views, massive concurrent handovers intensify the tension between individual access capacity and system-level fairness, bursty traffic and channel variation make timely coordination between slice budgets and medium access control (MAC)-layer scheduling difficult, and limited onboard computing resources restrict the use of high-complexity semantic transceiver models. Motivated by these constraints, this dissertation proposes a distributed cooperative resource-management method for end-to-end QoS assurance in large-scale LEO constellations. The method replaces global-state synchronization and centralized optimization with local state calibration, limited neighborhood interaction, and cross-node cooperation. Four key techniques are developed along the end-to-end service process: a multi-source confidence-regulated distributed local network-state awareness framework, a distributed cooperative access-handover mechanism based on local fairness estimation, a slice--MAC hierarchical cooperative optimization framework based on generative network situation awareness, and a lightweight semantic transmission mechanism based on task feedback and coordinated incremental retransmission.

(1) For network-state awareness, the first constraint on distributed routing is the trustworthiness of local state inputs. Degraded telemetry not only causes stale and missing node states, but also propagates unreliable information during multi-hop aggregation. This dissertation proposes a multi-source confidence-regulated distributed local network-state awareness framework, namely Confidence-Adaptive Local Distributed Processing (CA-LDP). CA-LDP incorporates confidence into both telemetry restoration and state propagation. In the restoration stage, stale and missing telemetry is recovered through confidence-adaptive graph-signal restoration according to information freshness, neighborhood spatial correlation, and current observation quality. In the aggregation stage, multi-hop state propagation is modeled through a graph reaction--diffusion process, and confidence-regulated graph-signal aggregation prevents low-confidence nodes from contaminating reliable local views. Distributed routing can therefore obtain trustworthy local network views without global-state collection. In a Starlink constellation with 1584 satellites and nine controlled telemetry-degradation scenarios, CA-LDP improves the average transmission rate by $4.8\%$--$10.8\%$, reduces the end-to-end delay by $6.7\%$--$11.1\%$, and maintains a routing success ratio close to $100\%$ over representative baselines.

(2) For user access handover, massive users select serving satellites concurrently without central coordination, and local rate gain can deviate from system-level long-term fairness. This dissertation proposes a distributed cooperative access-handover mechanism based on local fairness estimation, termed Fairness-Guided Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (FG-MAPPO). FG-MAPPO converts the generalized Gini social welfare objective into a locally estimated advantage correction exchanged within neighborhoods, and uses a dual-objective learning structure that separates throughput optimization from fairness coordination. The handover policy therefore preserves individual rate performance while sensing long-term fairness constraints. The cooperative training and execution procedure is compatible with the 3GPP Conditional Handover (CHO) process and jointly considers candidate-link quality, satellite load, and long-term access fairness using only local observations and limited neighborhood signaling. With neighborhood information exchange only, FG-MAPPO improves the generalized Gini fairness metric by $3.48\%$ and the average rate by $6.91\%$ compared with a centralized Hungarian algorithm with global information. Compared with distributed reinforcement-learning baselines, it improves the handover success ratio and load-balancing performance by up to $15.65\%$ and $30.91\%$, respectively.

(3) For radio-resource allocation, multi-user competition, bursty services, and channel variation make the impact of resource decisions on user-level QoS delayed and stochastic. Reactive scheduling based only on instantaneous queue and channel states cannot maintain long-term QoS feasibility. This dissertation proposes a slice--MAC hierarchical cooperative optimization framework based on generative network situation awareness, namely Dual-Timescale Predictive Scheduling (DTPS). DTPS uses bandwidth and power budgets as the cross-layer interface and QoS feasibility as the coordination criterion, decomposing long-term QoS assurance into slice-layer QoS feasible-region maintenance and MAC-layer real-time scheduling. At the slice layer, a world model generates future trajectories of traffic arrivals, queue evolution, and channel variation, predicts the support capability of budget configurations for rate--delay requirements, and determines slice bandwidth and power budgets in advance. At the MAC layer, QCI-priority knapsack scheduling estimates the minimum resource block group (RBG) and power cost required for each candidate flow to satisfy its rate--delay threshold, assigning scarce resources preferentially to flows whose QoS targets can be satisfied. In Starlink-scale simulations, DTPS achieves a user-level QoS satisfaction ratio of $80.8\%$ under overloaded traffic, substantially outperforming TDRL-RSUA, RL-JRSS, LACO, and DQN-MAC baselines with satisfaction ratios of $33.0\%$--$56.3\%$, while keeping the average delay at $78.3$~ms. When burst uncertainty increases, its QoS satisfaction ratio remains above $97.6\%$.

(4) For reliable semantic transmission, limited onboard computing resources make it difficult to improve task performance by increasing the complexity of semantic transceiver models. This dissertation proposes a lightweight semantic transmission mechanism based on task feedback and coordinated incremental retransmission, namely Coordinated Incremental Semantic HARQ (CIS-HARQ), shifting the reliability gain from model expansion to communication rounds and feedback cooperation. CIS-HARQ reformulates conventional full retransmission as task-feedback-guided selective incremental encoding and cross-round combining. The task-aware receiver generates feedback according to the gap between the current received semantic state and the task-discriminative features, the onboard transmitter generates rate-constrained semantic increments conditioned on this feedback, and the receiver progressively recovers task-relevant features through mask-guided gated semantic combining. The low-overhead implementation includes task-decision-margin attribution for semantic-block value ranking, entropy-based incremental-rate constraints, feedback-conditioned encoding through SFT at the transmitter, and gated semantic merging at the receiver. On the ImageNet-100, CIFAR-100, and VeRi-776 datasets over a Rician fading channel, at an SNR of $5$~dB and a tight channel bandwidth ratio (CBR) of about $0.02$, CIS-HARQ improves downstream task accuracy from $35.7\%$ for the equal-bandwidth baseline to $65.0\%$ after three incremental transmission rounds. With about $2.2$~M onboard parameters and millisecond-level inference latency, it reduces the parameter count by a factor of about $2500$ compared with a generative diffusion receiver, while keeping the task-accuracy gap within about $7\%$.

In summary, this dissertation builds a distributed closed-loop management framework covering trustworthy network-state awareness, fair user access, efficient radio-resource allocation, and low-overhead reliable semantic transmission. The four techniques form a progressive service-path relationship: trustworthy local state views support distributed routing; local fairness estimation constrains access handover; generative network situation awareness predicts how resource budgets support user-level QoS; and task feedback with coordinated incremental retransmission recovers task-relevant information under constrained links and onboard computing budgets. Constellation-scale simulations and semantic-transmission experiments show that the proposed methods improve network-state awareness reliability, access fairness, user-level QoS satisfaction under overloaded traffic, and task accuracy under tight bandwidth when reliable global states are unavailable and real-time ground control loops are limited. By accounting for limited neighborhood signaling, bounded onboard computing budgets, and protocol interfaces compatible with NR-NTN/CHO procedures, this dissertation advances resource management for large-scale LEO constellations from isolated layer-wise optimization to end-to-end QoS-oriented distributed cooperative management, and provides a verifiable technical path toward onboard edge autonomy in 6G space-air-ground integrated networks.
\end{abstract}
