\begin{abstract}[zh]
在 IMT-2030 所描绘的第六代移动通信（6G）愿景中，低轨（Low Earth Orbit，LEO）卫星网络凭借广覆盖、较低传输时延与高容量等特性，正成为支撑非地面接入、星间回传与全球连续服务的关键基础设施。随着小型化载荷与星上计算能力的发展，人工智能（Artificial Intelligence，AI）驱动的网络管控正由地面离线优化走向星上在线决策。然而，地面蜂窝网络依托稳定拓扑、可靠回传与可观测的全局状态实施集中管控，LEO 星座并不具备这些条件：其拓扑高速变化，遥测易退化，回传受限，星上资源紧张，难以直接沿用地面网络的集中式管控范式，管控方式正从地面集中调度转向星上自治与邻域协同。在此背景下，LEO 星座不能再仅充当透明转发管道，而需演进为具备服务质量（Quality of Service，QoS）保障能力的核心承载网，以支撑 6G 空天地一体化网络中的端到端业务承载。

面向 QoS 保障的端到端传输需要从全链路视角统筹感知、接入、资源分配与业务传输四个环节，并同时满足通信系统可靠性、资源分配效率和接入公平性等典型能力指标。基于 AI 的星上分布式管控以本地决策与邻域协同替代全局状态同步与集中求解，为此提供了可行的技术路径；然而在去中心化条件下，上述能力指标沿端到端路径具体化为四个难题。第一，遥测退化使局部观测的可用性与补全状态的可信度难以兼顾，恢复残差的不确定性还会沿多跳聚合扩散，节点难以据此构建可信的本地多跳网络视图。第二，海量用户在没有中心协调的条件下并发切换，个体切换效率与系统级长期公平性、负载均衡相互冲突。第三，多用户竞争与业务突发使资源决策的用户级 QoS 后果滞后且随机波动，受限的物理资源容易被配置给 QoS 不可达的用户，形成资源使用效率与用户级 QoS 保障之间的权衡。第四，星上资源受限，发送端难以通过增大模型规模提升语义传输性能，端到端传输质量与星上硬件开销之间矛盾突出。上述难题共同指向可靠性、资源分配效率与公平性三类关键能力。如何在缺乏可靠全局状态与中心化协调的条件下，沿端到端路径协同保障上述能力，便构成本文的核心问题。

围绕这一问题，本文提出一套可在星上在线执行的分布式协同资源管控方法，以本地状态校准、有限邻域交互与跨节点协作为基础，将端到端 QoS 保障沿感知、接入、资源分配与传输四个环节分解为可信状态感知、公平协作切换、切片—MAC 跨层资源分配与语义可靠传输四项关键技术，构成从状态获取、接入建立、资源分配到业务交付的闭环端到端传输保障体系。从设计目标看，四项技术分别面向可靠状态感知、个体切换效率与系统公平性的协同、资源使用效率与用户级 QoS 的协同，以及端到端传输性能与星上硬件开销的协同。本文的主要工作与创新点如下：

（1）在面向可靠状态感知的分布式路由方面，针对陈旧与缺失的邻居状态污染状态聚合中的可靠信息、削弱路由决策可靠性的问题，本文提出置信度自适应分布式处理（Confidence-Adaptive Local Distributed Processing，CA-LDP）框架，包含动态自适应的图信号恢复与置信度调制的图信号聚合两个环节。其中，置信度自适应图信号修复（Confidence-Adaptive Graph Signal Restoration，CaGSR）将退化遥测的恢复建模为时间先验、空间相关性与最新观测三类信息源之间的置信权重分配，据此校准陈旧与缺失的遥测数据；置信度加权多跳传播机制将状态聚合建模为图上的反应—扩散过程，由节点置信度调控信息扩散的强度：发送端按置信度控制自身状态向邻居扩散，接收端按置信度保持自身的可靠状态，抑制低置信节点在信息传播中对可靠节点的污染。在由 1584 颗卫星组成的真实 Starlink 星座与九类受控遥测退化场景下，CA-LDP 相较代表性基线将平均传输速率提升 $4.8\%$--$10.8\%$，将端到端时延降低 $6.7\%$--$11.1\%$，路由成功率保持在接近 $100\%$ 的水平，表明该框架能够在退化遥测条件下增强分布式路由的可靠性。

（2）在面向个体切换效率与系统公平性协同的移动性管理方面，针对海量用户基于局部观测并发切换时个体接入速率与系统级接入公平性相互冲突的问题，本文提出公平性引导的多智能体近端策略优化（Fairness-Guided Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization，FG-MAPPO）方法，将系统级公平目标转化为可由用户本地估计的优势修正量，在无全局协调的条件下实现公平感知的协作切换。该方法以广义 Gini 社会福利函数（Generalized Gini Social Welfare Function）统一刻画吞吐效率与接入公平性，构建多目标马尔可夫决策过程（Multi-Objective Markov Decision Process，MOMDP）并推导其策略梯度，证明各用户在邻域内广播经社会福利函数修正的个体优势估计可实现低开销的邻域公平性估计。在此基础上，本文设计吞吐与公平分立（Throughput-Oriented and Fairness-Oriented，TOFO）的网络结构与两阶段训练机制，降低多目标价值估计的方差，并构建与 3GPP 条件切换（Conditional Handover，CHO）流程兼容的分布式同步训练框架。仿真结果显示，在仅交互邻域信息的条件下，FG-MAPPO 的广义 Gini 公平性指标（Generalized Gini Fairness，GGF）较具备全局信息的集中式匈牙利算法提升 $3.48\%$，平均速率提升 $6.91\%$；相较分布式强化学习基线，其切换成功率与负载均衡性能最高分别提升 $15.65\%$ 与 $30.91\%$，验证了局部公平性估计与分布式协作切换的有效性。

（3）在面向资源使用效率与用户级 QoS 协同保障的无线资源分配方面，针对资源决策的用户级 QoS 后果滞后且随机波动、反应式调度下切片层与 MAC 层缺乏跨层协同的问题，本文提出双时间尺度预测调度（Dual-Timescale Predictive Scheduling，DTPS）框架，以带宽与功率预算为跨层接口、以 QoS 可行性为切片层与 MAC 层协同的统一准则，将长期 QoS 保障分解为切片层的 QoS 可行域保障与 MAC 层的实时调度。在切片层，基于世界模型（World Model）的生成式切片资源预留机制学习预算条件下的用户级 QoS 可行性动态：后验网络融合真实 MAC 层反馈校正隐空间表征，先验网络自记忆生成想象的未来切片轨迹，切片策略在想象轨迹上评估预算决策的长期 QoS 效应；在线执行时以最新 MAC 层反馈进行后验校正，缓解突发条件下的隐状态漂移，并以带宽与功率预算界定 MAC 层调度的 QoS 可行域。在 MAC 层，服务质量类别标识（QoS Class Identifier，QCI）优先级背包调度器在切片预算界定的可行域内执行 QoS 可行性感知的实时调度：先估计各候选流满足其速率—时延联合门限所需的最小资源块组（Resource Block Group，RBG）—功率开销并滤除不可行流，再以 QCI/5QI 导出的优先级为物品价值，经二维背包在固定切片预算下选取优先级加权的可行流子集，使有限无线资源优先服务于 QoS 可达业务。在 Starlink 规模的星座仿真中，DTPS 在过载业务下取得 $80.8\%$ 的用户级 QoS 满足率，明显高于代表性基线的 $33.0\%$--$56.3\%$，平均时延保持在 $78.3$~ms；突发不确定性增强时，其 QoS 满足率始终高于 $97.6\%$，验证了预测式预算控制与可行性感知跨层调度在业务与信道不确定性下对用户级长期 QoS 的保障能力。

（4）在面向端到端传输性能与星上硬件开销协同的可靠语义传输方面，针对星上载荷算力受限、星地链路呈现轻量发送端与任务感知接收端的非对称结构、难以为异构下游任务在星上部署任务专用编解码器的问题，本文遵循以扩展通信替代扩展星上计算的设计思路，提出协同增量语义混合自动重传请求（Coordinated Incremental Semantic Hybrid Automatic Repeat Request，CIS-HARQ）机制，将每一轮 HARQ 从完整语义重传重构为反馈引导的选择性语义精化，以反馈条件化的增量重传轮次换取模型容量。每一轮交互中，任务感知接收端在严格的反馈预算下，依据各语义分量对任务判决裕度的贡献定位最关键的分量，压缩为紧凑的 Top-$K$ 硬掩码，经低速率控制信令回传至发送端；发送端结合 HARQ 轮次嵌入，由反馈条件化增量编码器经空间特征变换生成码率受约束的选择性语义增量；接收端以门控软合并与掩码引导的语义累积仅更新被请求的分量，抑制含噪与冗余的增量，在前向链路、反馈链路与模型复杂度三重约束下逐步恢复任务相关语义。梯度式反馈生成部署于地面接收端，星上发送端仅保留轻量增量编码模型。在 ImageNet-100、CIFAR-100 与 VeRi-776 数据集、Rician 衰落信道、信噪比 $5$ dB 且信道带宽比（Channel Bandwidth Ratio，CBR）约为 $0.02$ 的紧带宽条件下，CIS-HARQ 经三轮增量传输将下游任务精度从同带宽基线的 $35.7\%$ 提升至 $65.0\%$；其参数量仅约 $2.2$ M，星上推理时延为毫秒级，相较生成式扩散接收端参数量降低约 $2500$ 倍、推理时延由秒级缩短至毫秒级，任务精度差距控制在约 $7$ 个百分点以内，且在不同数据分布与多任务接收端上保持一致的精化增益。

综上，本文以端到端 QoS 保障为主线，从全链路传输过程将问题分解为感知、接入、资源分配与传输四个环节，构建了覆盖可信感知、公平接入、高效资源分配与可靠传输的闭环管控体系：可信网络状态感知提升分布式路由所依赖的状态可靠性，公平协作切换协调个体切换效率与系统长期公平性，双时间尺度预测调度协调资源使用效率与用户级 QoS 保障，协同增量语义 HARQ 协调端到端传输性能与星上硬件开销。星座规模的仿真与语义传输实验表明，所提机制在网络状态可信感知、接入公平性、过载业务下的用户级 QoS 满足率以及紧带宽下的任务精度等方面均有明显的性能提升。本文将大规模 LEO 星座的资源管控由分层独立优化推进至面向端到端 QoS 的分布式协同管控，为 6G 空天地一体化网络的星上边缘自治提供了理论方法与可验证的技术路径。

\end{abstract}

\begin{abstract}[en]
In the IMT-2030 vision of sixth-generation (6G) mobile communications, low Earth orbit (LEO) satellite networks are becoming a key infrastructure for non-terrestrial access, inter-satellite backhaul, and globally continuous services, owing to their wide coverage, relatively low transmission latency, and high capacity. As miniaturized payloads and onboard computing mature, artificial intelligence (AI)-driven network management is moving from ground-based offline optimization to onboard online decision-making. Terrestrial cellular networks, however, perform centralized control on the strength of stable topologies, reliable backhaul, and observable global states. LEO constellations offer none of these conditions: the topology changes rapidly, telemetry is prone to degradation, backhaul is constrained, and onboard resources are scarce, so the centralized paradigm of terrestrial networks cannot be transplanted directly, and constellation management is shifting from ground-centric centralized scheduling toward onboard autonomy with neighborhood-level coordination. Against this background, an LEO constellation can no longer act as a transparent forwarding pipe; it has to evolve into a core bearer network with quality-of-service (QoS) assurance capabilities to support end-to-end service provisioning in 6G space-air-ground integrated networks.

End-to-end transmission with QoS assurance has to be examined from the full transmission chain, including perception, access, resource allocation, and service delivery, while meeting the representative capability requirements of communication systems: reliability, resource-allocation efficiency, and access fairness. AI-based onboard distributed management, which replaces global-state synchronization and centralized computation with local decisions and neighborhood coordination, offers a viable path toward this goal. Under decentralized control, however, these requirements materialize along the end-to-end path as four difficulties. First, degraded telemetry forces a compromise between the availability of local observations and the trustworthiness of the restored states, and the uncertainty of the restoration residual spreads through multi-hop aggregation, so a node can hardly build a trustworthy local multi-hop view of the network. Second, when massive users hand over concurrently without central coordination, individual handover efficiency conflicts with system-level long-term fairness and load balancing. Third, multi-user contention and traffic bursts make the user-level QoS consequence of a resource decision delayed and stochastic; the limited physical resources are easily granted to users whose QoS targets are unattainable, creating a tension between resource-use efficiency and user-level QoS assurance. Fourth, with scarce onboard resources, the transmitter cannot improve semantic transmission performance simply by enlarging the model, so end-to-end transmission quality conflicts with onboard hardware overhead. The core problem of this dissertation is therefore how to jointly guarantee reliability, efficiency, and fairness along the end-to-end path when neither reliable global states nor centralized coordination is available.

To this end, this dissertation develops a set of distributed cooperative resource-management methods that run online onboard satellites. Built on local state calibration, limited neighborhood interaction, and cross-node cooperation, the methods decompose end-to-end QoS assurance along the perception, access, resource-allocation, and transmission stages into four key techniques: trustworthy state perception, fair cooperative handover, slice--MAC cross-layer resource allocation, and reliable semantic transmission. Together they form a closed-loop end-to-end transmission-assurance framework from state acquisition, access establishment, and resource allocation to service delivery. In terms of design objectives, the four techniques address trustworthy state perception, the balance between individual handover efficiency and system fairness, the balance between resource-use efficiency and user-level QoS assurance, and the balance between end-to-end transmission performance and onboard hardware overhead, respectively. The main contributions are summarized as follows.

(1) For reliable network-state perception in distributed routing, to address the problem that stale and missing neighbor states contaminate the reliable information during state aggregation and weaken routing decisions, this dissertation proposes a Confidence-Adaptive Local Distributed Processing (CA-LDP) framework that couples dynamically adaptive graph-signal restoration with confidence-modulated graph-signal aggregation. Within CA-LDP, Confidence-Adaptive Graph Signal Restoration (CaGSR) casts the recovery of degraded telemetry as an allocation of confidence weights among three information sources, i.e., the temporal prior, the spatial correlation, and the latest observation, and calibrates stale and missing telemetry accordingly. A confidence-weighted multi-hop propagation mechanism models state aggregation as a reaction--diffusion process on the graph, in which node confidence regulates the diffusion intensity: a sender scales the diffusion of its own state to neighbors according to its confidence, and a receiver retains its own reliable state according to its confidence, which suppresses the contamination of reliable nodes by low-confidence ones during information propagation. On a real Starlink constellation of 1584 satellites under nine controlled telemetry-degradation scenarios, CA-LDP raises the average transmission rate by $4.8\%$--$10.8\%$ and reduces the end-to-end delay by $6.7\%$--$11.1\%$ over representative baselines while keeping the routing success ratio close to $100\%$, which confirms that the framework strengthens the reliability of distributed routing under degraded telemetry.

(2) For mobility management that balances individual handover efficiency and system fairness, to address the conflict between individual access rate and system-level access fairness under massive concurrent handovers driven by local observations, this dissertation proposes Fairness-Guided Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (FG-MAPPO), which converts the system-level fairness objective into an advantage-correction term that each user estimates locally, and so achieves fairness-aware cooperative handover without global coordination. The method adopts a Generalized Gini Social Welfare Function to jointly characterize throughput efficiency and access fairness, formulates a Multi-Objective Markov Decision Process (MOMDP), and derives its policy gradient, proving that broadcasting the social-welfare-corrected individual advantage estimate within the neighborhood achieves low-overhead estimation of neighborhood fairness. On this basis, a Throughput-Oriented and Fairness-Oriented (TOFO) network architecture with a two-stage training mechanism reduces the variance of multi-objective value estimation, and a distributed synchronous training framework keeps the design compatible with the 3GPP Conditional Handover (CHO) procedure. Simulations show that, with neighborhood information exchange only, FG-MAPPO improves the Generalized Gini Fairness (GGF) metric by $3.48\%$ and the average rate by $6.91\%$ over a centralized Hungarian algorithm with global information; against distributed reinforcement-learning baselines, it improves the handover success rate and the load-balancing performance by up to $15.65\%$ and $30.91\%$, respectively, which verifies the effectiveness of local fairness estimation and distributed cooperative handover.

(3) For radio-resource allocation that coordinates resource-use efficiency and user-level QoS assurance, to address the delayed and stochastic user-level QoS consequences of resource decisions and the absence of slice--MAC cross-layer coordination under reactive scheduling, this dissertation proposes a Dual-Timescale Predictive Scheduling (DTPS) framework, which takes bandwidth and power budgets as the cross-layer interface, adopts QoS feasibility as the unified criterion for slice--MAC coordination, and decomposes long-term QoS assurance into QoS feasible-region assurance at the slice layer and real-time scheduling at the MAC layer. At the slice layer, a world-model-based generative slice resource reservation mechanism learns the budget-conditioned dynamics of user-level QoS feasibility: a posterior network absorbs real MAC-layer feedback to calibrate the latent representation, a prior network generates imagined future slice trajectories from memory, and the slicing policy evaluates the long-term QoS effect of a budget decision on the imagined trajectories. During online execution, posterior correction with the latest MAC-layer feedback mitigates latent-state drift under bursty conditions, and the bandwidth and power budgets delimit the QoS feasible region for MAC-layer scheduling. At the MAC layer, a QoS Class Identifier (QCI) priority knapsack scheduler performs QoS-feasibility-aware real-time scheduling within the feasible region delimited by the slice budgets: it first estimates the minimum resource block group (RBG) and power cost for each candidate flow to meet its joint rate--delay threshold and filters out infeasible flows, and then solves a two-dimensional knapsack with QCI/5QI-derived priorities as item values to select a priority-weighted subset of feasible flows under the fixed slice budgets, so that limited radio resources are directed to QoS-attainable flows. In Starlink-scale simulations, DTPS reaches a user-level QoS satisfaction ratio of $80.8\%$ under overloaded traffic, well above the $33.0\%$--$56.3\%$ of representative baselines, while the average delay is held at $78.3$~ms; as burst-induced uncertainty grows, the satisfaction ratio stays above $97.6\%$, which verifies that predictive budget control and feasibility-aware cross-layer scheduling sustain long-term user-level QoS under traffic and channel uncertainty.

(4) For reliable semantic transmission that coordinates end-to-end transmission performance and onboard hardware overhead, to address the limited onboard computing capability, the asymmetric space--ground structure in which a lightweight transmitter only acquires and forwards imagery while heterogeneous downstream tasks run at a task-aware receiver, and the impracticality of hosting task-specific codecs onboard, this dissertation follows the principle of scaling communication rather than onboard computation and proposes a Coordinated Incremental Semantic Hybrid Automatic Repeat Request (CIS-HARQ) mechanism, which recasts each HARQ round from a retransmission of the complete semantic representation into feedback-guided selective semantic refinement and trades feedback-conditioned incremental retransmission rounds for model capacity. In each round, the task-aware receiver, under a stringent feedback budget, locates the semantic components most critical to the task decision margin, compresses them into a compact Top-$K$ hard mask, and returns the mask to the transmitter over a low-rate control channel. Conditioned on this mask and an HARQ-round embedding, the transmitter generates rate-constrained selective semantic increments through a feedback-conditioned incremental encoder with spatial feature transform. The receiver applies gated soft combining and mask-guided semantic accumulation to update only the requested components, suppresses noisy and redundant increments, and recovers task-relevant semantics progressively under the joint constraints of the forward link, the feedback link, and the model complexity. The gradient-based feedback generation is deployed at the ground receiver, and the onboard transmitter retains only a lightweight incremental encoder. On the ImageNet-100, CIFAR-100, and VeRi-776 datasets over a Rician fading channel, at an SNR of $5$ dB and a tight channel bandwidth ratio (CBR) of about $0.02$, CIS-HARQ lifts the downstream task accuracy from $35.7\%$ for the equal-bandwidth baseline to $65.0\%$ after three incremental rounds. With only about $2.2$~M parameters and millisecond-level onboard inference, it cuts the parameter count by a factor of about $2500$ relative to a generative diffusion-based receiver and shortens the inference time from seconds to milliseconds, while the task-accuracy gap stays within about $7$ percentage points and the refinement gain remains consistent across data distributions and multi-task receivers.

In summary, this dissertation takes end-to-end QoS assurance as its main thread, decomposes the full transmission chain into perception, access, resource allocation, and transmission, and builds a closed-loop management framework that spans trustworthy perception, fair access, efficient resource allocation, and reliable transmission. Trustworthy network-state perception improves the state reliability required by distributed routing; fair cooperative handover coordinates individual handover efficiency and system-level long-term fairness; dual-timescale predictive scheduling coordinates resource-use efficiency and user-level QoS assurance; and coordinated incremental semantic HARQ coordinates end-to-end transmission performance and onboard hardware overhead. Constellation-scale simulations and semantic transmission experiments show clear gains in trustworthy network-state perception, access fairness, user-level QoS satisfaction under overloaded traffic, and task accuracy under tight bandwidth. This dissertation advances the resource management of large-scale LEO constellations from layered, isolated optimization to end-to-end QoS-oriented distributed cooperative management, and offers theoretical methods together with a verifiable technical path toward onboard edge autonomy in 6G space-air-ground integrated networks.
\end{abstract}
