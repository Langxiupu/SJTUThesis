\begin{abstract}[zh]
在 IMT-2030 描绘的第六代移动通信（6G）愿景中，低轨（Low Earth Orbit，LEO）卫星网络凭借广覆盖、较低传输时延与高容量等特性，正成为支撑非地面接入、星间回传与全球连续服务的关键基础设施。随着小型化载荷与星上计算能力的发展，人工智能（Artificial Intelligence，AI）驱动的网络管控正由地面离线优化向星上在线决策演进，卫星网络管控模式也由地面集中式调度逐步转向星上自治与邻域协同。在此趋势下，LEO 星座不再仅作为透明转发管道，而需演进为具备服务质量（Quality of Service，QoS）保障能力的自治网络，以支撑 6G 空天地一体化网络中的端到端业务承载。

面向 QoS 的端到端传输可概括为状态感知与业务传输可靠性、受限资源下的资源配置效率，以及多用户竞争下的接入公平性三类关键维度的联合保障。可靠的网络状态感知与业务传输是资源调度、路由决策和接入控制的基础，资源效率与接入公平性则构成资源受限条件下的主要权衡。地面网络凭借稳定拓扑、可靠回程与可观测全局状态，可以实施集中式跨层优化；而在大规模 LEO 星座中，高速运动、遥测退化、不可靠回程与星上资源受限使节点难以实时获得可靠的全局网络视图。同时，联合优化的决策空间随星座规模、用户规模与资源维度快速增长，集中求解结果又会因拓扑与信道的快速变化而迅速失效。因此，依赖全局状态同步与集中计算的跨层优化在星座尺度上既难以求解，也难以部署。如何在缺乏可靠全局状态与中心化协调的条件下，沿端到端路径协同保障可靠性、效率与公平性，构成本文研究的核心问题。

为此，本文提出一套可在星上在线执行的分布式协同资源管控方法体系，以本地状态校准、有限邻域交互与跨节点协作为基础，降低对全局状态同步与中心化决策的依赖，并沿“感知—接入—调度—传输”链路将端到端 QoS 保障分解为四项相互衔接的关键技术。网络状态感知与语义可靠传输分别从路由状态输入侧和端到端业务传输侧保障可靠性，协作切换与跨层调度在接入侧和资源侧协调公平性与效率，共同构成面向大规模 LEO 星座的闭环管控体系。本文的主要工作与创新点如下。

（1）在面向分布式路由的网络状态感知方面，针对退化遥测导致局部状态陈旧、缺失且置信度不一致，进而削弱路由决策可靠性的问题，提出置信度自适应局部分布式处理（Confidence-Adaptive Local Distributed Processing，CA-LDP）框架。该框架将校准后置信度贯穿状态校准与多跳聚合全过程，克服现有方法中状态恢复与特征聚合相互解耦、残留状态不确定性在多跳聚合中扩散的不足。具体而言，置信度自适应图信号修复（Confidence-Adaptive Graph Signal Restoration，CaGSR）通过观测—先验参考机制校准陈旧与缺失遥测，并生成校准后置信度；置信度加权多跳传播机制进一步在发送端抑制弱支撑状态的向外传播，在接收端锚定可靠拥塞证据，使路由节点在利用局部观测的同时显式区分不同状态证据的可信程度。在 Starlink 壳层星座的九类受控遥测退化场景下，CA-LDP 相较代表性基线将平均传输速率提升 $4.8\%$--$10.8\%$，端到端时延降低 $6.7\%$--$11.1\%$，并保持接近饱和的业务传输成功率，验证了置信度耦合机制在退化遥测条件下对分布式路由的鲁棒增强作用。

（2）在接入与移动性管理方面，针对随机接入缺乏全局协调、海量用户并发切换引发接入竞争与公平性失衡的问题，提出公平引导的多智能体近端策略优化（Fairness-Guided Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization，FG-MAPPO）方法。该方法将系统级公平目标转化为可由用户本地估计的优势修正量，从而在无全局协调条件下实现公平感知的协作切换。具体而言，本文以广义 Gini 函数（Generalized Gini Function，GGF）对吞吐效率与接入公平性进行统一刻画，构建多目标马尔可夫决策过程（Multi-Objective Markov Decision Process，MOMDP），并推导多目标策略梯度的解析形式。基于该推导结果，证明各用户仅需在邻域内广播经 GGF 修正的个体优势估计，即可实现对邻域公平性的低开销估计。在此基础上，设计吞吐与公平分立（Throughput-Oriented and Fairness-Oriented，TOFO）网络结构与两阶段训练机制，以降低多目标价值估计方差，并构建与 3GPP 条件切换（Conditional Handover，CHO）兼容的分布式同步训练框架。仿真结果表明，在仅交互邻域信息的条件下，FG-MAPPO 的 GGF 公平性指标较具备全局信息的集中式匈牙利算法提升 $3.48\%$，平均速率提升 $6.91\%$；相较分布式强化学习基线，其切换成功率与负载均衡性能最高分别提升 $15.65\%$ 和 $30.91\%$，验证了局部公平估计与分布式协作切换机制的有效性。

（3）在无线资源跨层调度方面，针对部分可观测、随机业务突发与时变服务能力下切片级预算控制与 MAC 级无线资源分配难以协同，导致用户级 QoS 难以保障的问题，提出双时间尺度预测调度（Dual-Timescale Predictive Scheduling，DTPS）框架。该框架以环境演化预测模型（World Model）学习 QoS 可行性状态的演化规律，而非仅预测业务负载，从而将难以直接求解的跨层混合整数优化问题转化为慢时间尺度预算控制与快时间尺度可行性感知调度的协同设计。具体而言，在切片层，先验—后验状态空间预测模型结合在线后验校正，在观测不完整与业务突发条件下生成具备前瞻性的带宽与功率预算决策；在 MAC 层，基于服务质量类别标识（QoS Class Identifier，QCI）优先级的 RBG--功率联合调度器，将切片层预算转化为资源块组（Resource Block Group，RBG）与功率的联合分配，并通过最小 RBG--功率可行性判定降低资源消耗。该机制以优先级加权 QoS 满足率为优化目标，在资源受限条件下提升用户级 QoS 保障能力和无线资源利用效率。在 Starlink 规模化仿真中，DTPS 在过载业务场景下实现 $80.8\%$ 的用户级 QoS 满足率，显著高于代表性方法的 $33.0\%$--$56.3\%$；在业务突发强度变化时，QoS 满足率波动低于 $0.1$ 个百分点，表明该框架在非平稳业务条件下具备较好的调度稳定性。

（4）在受限条件下的语义可靠传输方面，针对星上算力受限、业务输入数据异构与完整语义表征重传开销过高，导致语义反馈与重传难以低开销实现的问题，提出协同增量语义混合自动重传请求（Coordinated Incremental Semantic Hybrid Automatic Repeat Request，CIS-HARQ）机制。该机制构建由接收端语义失真驱动发送端选择性增量编码的闭环传输过程，使发送端无需预知具体任务类型，也无需部署大参数量或专用语义评估模型，即可实现可靠语义传输。具体而言，接收端依据语义失真生成 Top-$K$ 硬掩码反馈，并通过低速控制信令回传至发送端；发送端基于该反馈，利用反馈条件化增量编码器与 HARQ 轮次嵌入生成选择性语义增量，避免重复发送完整语义表征；接收端进一步通过掩码引导的语义累积机制，按失真掩码融合多轮语义增量，在有限带宽与星上计算资源下逐步提升语义表征质量。在 ImageNet-100 数据集、信噪比 $5$ dB、信道带宽比（Channel Bandwidth Ratio，CBR）约为 $0.02$ 的紧带宽条件下，CIS-HARQ 经三轮增量传输将下游任务精度由同带宽基线的 $35.7\%$ 提升至 $65.0\%$，且模型规模与传输时延均低于生成式基线。

综上，本文围绕大规模 LEO 星座在缺乏可靠全局状态与中心化协调条件下的端到端 QoS 保障问题，以分布式协同决策为主线，构建了覆盖可信感知、公平接入、高效调度与可靠传输的闭环管控体系。四项关键技术沿端到端路径形成递进支撑关系：可信网络状态感知为分布式路由及上层调度提供本地多跳视图，构成在线决策的状态基础；公平协作切换为终端提供连续且公平的接入，支撑后续资源调度与业务传输；双时间尺度预测调度将切片预算转化为资源受限下的用户级 QoS 保障能力；语义可靠传输则在受限链路上以低开销提升任务相关信息的传输可靠性。星座规模仿真与语义传输实验表明，所提机制在网络状态可信感知、接入公平性、过载业务下用户级 QoS 满足率与紧带宽下任务精度等方面均取得显著增益。本文将大规模 LEO 星座资源管控由分层独立优化推进至面向端到端 QoS 的分布式协同管控，为 6G 空天地一体化网络的星上边缘自治提供理论方法与可验证的技术路径。

\end{abstract}

\begin{abstract}[en]
In the IMT-2030 vision of sixth-generation mobile communications (6G), low Earth orbit (LEO) satellite networks, owing to their wide coverage, relatively low transmission latency, and high capacity, are becoming a key infrastructure for non-terrestrial access, inter-satellite backhaul, and globally continuous services. With the development of miniaturized payloads and onboard computing capabilities, artificial intelligence (AI)-driven network control is evolving from ground-based offline optimization toward onboard online decision-making, and the control paradigm of satellite networks is shifting from ground-centric centralized scheduling to onboard autonomy and neighborhood cooperation. Under this trend, LEO constellations are no longer expected to serve merely as transparent forwarding pipes, but need to evolve into autonomous networks with quality-of-service (QoS) assurance capability, so as to support end-to-end service provisioning in 6G space-air-ground integrated networks.

End-to-end transmission with QoS assurance can be summarized as the joint guarantee of three key dimensions: the reliability of state perception and service transmission, the resource allocation efficiency under constrained resources, and the access fairness under multi-user contention. Reliable network-state perception and service transmission provide the basis for resource scheduling, routing decisions, and access control, while resource efficiency and access fairness constitute the main tradeoff under resource constraints. In terrestrial networks, stable topology, reliable backhaul, and observable global states make centralized cross-layer optimization feasible. In large-scale LEO constellations, however, high mobility, degraded telemetry, unreliable backhaul, and limited onboard resources make it difficult for nodes to obtain a reliable global network view in real time. Meanwhile, the decision space of joint optimization grows rapidly with the constellation scale, user population, and resource dimensions, and the centralized solution may quickly become outdated due to fast-varying topology and channel conditions. Therefore, cross-layer optimization relying on global-state synchronization and centralized computation is neither tractable nor deployable at constellation scale. How to jointly guarantee reliability, efficiency, and fairness along the end-to-end path in the absence of reliable global states and centralized coordination constitutes the core problem addressed in this dissertation.

To address this problem, this dissertation develops a distributed cooperative resource management framework that can be executed online onboard satellites. Based on local state calibration, limited neighborhood interaction, and cross-node cooperation, the proposed framework reduces the dependence on global-state synchronization and centralized decision-making. Along the ``perception--access--scheduling--transmission'' chain, end-to-end QoS assurance is decomposed into four interconnected key techniques. Network-state perception and semantic-reliable transmission ensure reliability from the routing-state input side and the end-to-end service transmission side, respectively, while cooperative handover and cross-layer scheduling coordinate fairness and efficiency at the access and resource-control sides. Together, they form a closed-loop management framework for large-scale LEO constellations. The main contributions and innovations of this dissertation are summarized as follows.

(1) For network-state perception in distributed routing, to address the problem that stale, missing, and confidence-inconsistent local states caused by degraded telemetry weaken the reliability of routing decisions, a Confidence-Adaptive Local Distributed Processing (CA-LDP) framework is proposed. The framework carries post-calibration confidence throughout the state calibration and multi-hop aggregation process, overcoming the limitation of existing methods where state restoration and feature aggregation are decoupled and residual state uncertainty may diffuse during multi-hop aggregation. Specifically, Confidence-Adaptive Graph Signal Restoration (CaGSR) calibrates stale and missing telemetry through an observation--prior reference mechanism and generates post-calibration confidence. A confidence-weighted multi-hop propagation mechanism further suppresses the outward propagation of weakly supported states at the sender side and anchors reliable congestion evidence at the receiver side, allowing routing nodes to exploit local observations while explicitly distinguishing the credibility of different state evidence. Under nine controlled telemetry-degradation scenarios on a Starlink-shell constellation, CA-LDP improves the average transmission rate by $4.8\%$--$10.8\%$, reduces the end-to-end delay by $6.7\%$--$11.1\%$, and maintains a near-saturated service delivery ratio compared with representative baselines, validating the robustness enhancement brought by confidence coupling for distributed routing under degraded telemetry.

(2) For access and mobility management, to address access contention and fairness imbalance caused by massive concurrent handovers under random access without global coordination, a Fairness-Guided Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (FG-MAPPO) method is proposed. The method converts the system-level fairness objective into an advantage correction term that can be locally estimated by users, thereby enabling fairness-aware cooperative handover without global coordination. Specifically, a Generalized Gini Function (GGF) is used to jointly characterize throughput efficiency and access fairness, and a Multi-Objective Markov Decision Process (MOMDP) is formulated. The analytical form of the multi-objective policy gradient is then derived. Based on this derivation, it is shown that each user only needs to broadcast its GGF-corrected individual advantage estimate within its neighborhood to achieve low-overhead estimation of neighborhood fairness. Furthermore, a Throughput-Oriented and Fairness-Oriented (TOFO) network architecture and a two-stage training mechanism are designed to reduce the variance of multi-objective value estimation, and a distributed synchronous training framework compatible with the 3GPP Conditional Handover (CHO) procedure is constructed. Simulation results show that, with only neighborhood information exchange, FG-MAPPO improves the GGF fairness metric by $3.48\%$ and the average rate by $6.91\%$ compared with a centralized Hungarian algorithm with global information. Compared with distributed reinforcement-learning baselines, it improves the handover success rate and load-balancing performance by up to $15.65\%$ and $30.91\%$, respectively, validating the effectiveness of local fairness estimation and distributed cooperative handover.

(3) For cross-layer radio resource scheduling, to address the difficulty of coordinating slice-level budget control and MAC-level radio resource allocation under partial observability, bursty traffic, and time-varying service capability, which makes user-level QoS difficult to guarantee, a Dual-Timescale Predictive Scheduling (DTPS) framework is proposed. The framework learns the evolution of QoS feasibility states through an environment-evolution prediction model, or world model, rather than merely predicting traffic load, thereby transforming the hard-to-solve cross-layer mixed-integer optimization problem into a cooperative design between slow-timescale budget control and fast-timescale feasibility-aware scheduling. Specifically, at the slice layer, a prior--posterior state-space prediction model with online posterior correction generates foresighted bandwidth and power budget decisions under incomplete observations and bursty traffic. At the MAC layer, a QoS Class Identifier (QCI)-priority-based RBG--power joint scheduler maps the slice-level budget into joint allocation of resource block groups (RBGs) and power, and reduces resource consumption through a minimum RBG--power feasibility test. With the priority-weighted QoS satisfaction ratio as the optimization objective, this mechanism improves user-level QoS assurance and radio resource utilization efficiency under resource constraints. In Starlink-scale simulations, DTPS achieves a user-level QoS satisfaction ratio of $80.8\%$ under overloaded traffic, significantly outperforming representative baselines with $33.0\%$--$56.3\%$. When the traffic burst intensity varies, the fluctuation of the QoS satisfaction ratio remains below $0.1$ percentage points, indicating good scheduling stability under non-stationary traffic conditions.

(4) For semantic-reliable transmission under resource constraints, to address the difficulty of low-overhead semantic feedback and retransmission under limited onboard computing, heterogeneous service inputs, and excessive retransmission overhead of complete semantic representations, a Coordinated Incremental Semantic Hybrid Automatic Repeat Request (CIS-HARQ) mechanism is proposed. The mechanism establishes a closed-loop transmission process in which receiver-side semantic distortion drives transmitter-side selective incremental encoding, enabling reliable semantic transmission without requiring the transmitter to know the specific downstream task type or to deploy a large-scale or task-specific semantic evaluation model. Specifically, the receiver generates Top-$K$ hard-mask feedback according to semantic distortion and returns it to the transmitter through a low-rate control channel. Based on this feedback, the transmitter uses a feedback-conditioned incremental encoder and HARQ-round embedding to generate selective semantic increments, avoiding repeated transmission of the complete semantic representation. The receiver then employs a mask-guided semantic accumulation mechanism to fuse multi-round semantic increments according to the distortion mask, progressively improving semantic representation quality under limited bandwidth and onboard computing resources. On the ImageNet-100 dataset, under an SNR of $5$ dB and a tight channel bandwidth ratio (CBR) of approximately $0.02$, CIS-HARQ improves the downstream task accuracy from $35.7\%$ for the equal-bandwidth baseline to $65.0\%$ after three incremental transmission rounds, while achieving lower model size and transmission latency than the generative baseline.

In summary, this dissertation addresses end-to-end QoS assurance for large-scale LEO constellations in the absence of reliable global states and centralized coordination. With distributed cooperative decision-making as the main thread, it develops a closed-loop management framework covering reliable state perception, fair access, efficient scheduling, and reliable transmission. The four key techniques form a progressive support relationship along the end-to-end path: reliable network-state perception provides a local multi-hop view for distributed routing and upper-layer scheduling, serving as the state basis for online decision-making; fairness-aware cooperative handover provides users with continuous and fair access, supporting subsequent resource scheduling and service transmission; dual-timescale predictive scheduling translates slice-level budgets into user-level QoS assurance under resource constraints; and semantic-reliable transmission improves the reliability of task-relevant information over constrained links with low overhead. Constellation-scale simulations and semantic transmission experiments show that the proposed mechanisms achieve significant gains in reliable network-state perception, access fairness, user-level QoS satisfaction under overloaded traffic, and task accuracy under tight bandwidth. This dissertation advances resource management for large-scale LEO constellations from layered and isolated optimization toward end-to-end QoS-oriented distributed cooperative management, providing theoretical methods and a verifiable technical path for onboard edge autonomy in 6G space-air-ground integrated networks.
\end{abstract}
