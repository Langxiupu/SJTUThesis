\begin{abstract}[zh]
在 IMT-2030 描绘的第六代移动通信（6G）愿景中，低轨（Low Earth Orbit，LEO）卫星网络凭借广覆盖、较低传输时延与高容量等特性，正成为支撑非地面接入、星间回传与全球连续服务的关键基础设施。随着小型化载荷与星上计算能力的发展，人工智能（Artificial Intelligence，AI）驱动的网络管控正由地面离线优化向星上在线决策演进。在此趋势下，LEO 星座不再仅作为透明转发管道，而需演进为具备服务质量（Quality of Service，QoS）保障能力的核心承载网，以支撑 6G 空天地一体化网络中的端到端业务承载。

面向 QoS 的端到端传输需要状态感知与业务传输可靠性、受限资源下的资源配置效率以及多用户竞争下的接入公平性三类维度的联合保障。基于 AI 的星上分布式管控以本地决策与邻域协同替代全局状态同步与集中求解，为此提供了有前景的技术范式，但在去中心化条件下，上述三类维度沿“感知—接入—调度—传输”路径具化为四重困难：其一，退化遥测使局部观测可用性与补全状态可信度难以兼得，且恢复残差的不确定性沿多跳聚合扩散，节点难以构建可信的本地多跳网络视图；其二，无中心协调的海量并发切换使个体短期接入收益与系统长期公平性、负载均衡相互冲突，须在受限邻域信令下在线化解；其三，慢时间尺度的切片级预算与毫秒级 MAC 层细粒度资源映射存在粒度—时间尺度双重失配，切片配额难以转化为用户级 QoS 保障；其四，发送端任务先验缺失叠加反馈、带宽与算力受限，使低开销重传触发、特征级增量编码与接收端低复杂度语义合并的闭环协同难以实现。上述四重困难分别对应状态可靠性、接入公平性、资源效率与传输可靠性。由此，如何在缺乏可靠全局状态与中心化协调的条件下沿端到端路径协同保障可靠性、效率与公平性，构成本文的核心问题。为此，本文提出一套可在星上在线执行的分布式协同资源管控方法体系，以本地状态校准、有限邻域交互与跨节点协作为基础，将端到端 QoS 保障沿“感知—接入—调度—传输”分解为可信状态感知、公平协作切换、切片—MAC 跨层调度与语义可靠传输四项关键技术，共同构成面向大规模 LEO 星座的闭环管控体系。本文的主要工作与创新点如下：

（1）面向鲁棒性的网络状态感知方面，邻居状态的陈旧与缺失使状态聚合中的可靠信息被污染，削弱路由决策的可靠性。为此，本文提出置信度自适应分布式处理（Confidence-Adaptive Local Distributed Processing，CA-LDP）框架，实现动态自适应的图信号恢复与置信度调制的图信号聚合。其中，置信度自适应图信号修复（Confidence-Adaptive Graph Signal Restoration，CaGSR）将退化遥测的恢复建模为时间先验、空间相关性与最新观测三类信息源之间的信用分配，在时空相关性与信息新鲜度之间权衡，据此校准陈旧与缺失遥测。为抑制信息传播过程中低置信节点对可靠节点的污染，置信度加权多跳传播机制将状态聚合建模为图上的反应—扩散过程，由节点置信度调控信息扩散强度，使发送端按置信度控制状态向邻居扩散的强度、接收端按置信度保持自身可靠状态。在由 1584 颗卫星组成的 Starlink 真实星座的九类受控遥测退化场景下，CA-LDP 相较代表性基线将平均传输速率提升 $4.8\%$--$10.8\%$，端到端时延降低 $6.7\%$--$11.1\%$，并实现接近$100\%$的路由成功率。结果表明，CA-LDP 可在退化遥测条件下增强分布式路由的鲁棒性。

（2）面向服务连续性保障的移动性管理方面，海量用户基于局部观测的并发切换使用户个体容量最大化与系统级接入公平性相互冲突。为此，本文提出公平性引导的多智能体近端策略优化（Fairness-Guided Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization，FG-MAPPO）方法，将系统级公平目标转化为可由用户本地估计的优势修正量，从而在无全局协调的条件下实现公平感知的协作切换。该方法以广义 Gini 社会福利函数（Generalized Gini Social Welfare Function）统一刻画吞吐效率与接入公平性，构建多目标马尔可夫决策过程（Multi-Objective Markov Decision Process，MOMDP）并推导其策略梯度，证明各用户仅需在邻域内广播经社会福利函数修正的个体优势估计，即可低开销地估计邻域公平性。在此基础上，本文设计吞吐与公平分立（Throughput-Oriented and Fairness-Oriented，TOFO）网络结构与两阶段训练机制以降低多目标价值估计的方差，并构建与 3GPP 条件切换（Conditional Handover，CHO）兼容的分布式同步训练框架。仿真结果表明，仅交互邻域信息时，FG-MAPPO 的广义 Gini 公平性指标（Generalized Gini Fairness，GGF）较具备全局信息的集中式匈牙利算法提升 $3.48\%$，平均速率提升 $6.91\%$。相较分布式强化学习基线，其切换成功率与负载均衡性能最高分别提升 $15.65\%$ 和 $30.91\%$。上述结果验证了局部公平估计与分布式协作切换的有效性。

（3）面向资源高效的用户级 QoS 保障方面，网络的动态性以及多用户竞争使长期系统动态难以刻画，切片层预算与 MAC 层调度难以协同，长期用户级 QoS 难以保障。针对该问题，本文提出双时间尺度预测调度（Dual-Timescale Predictive Scheduling，DTPS）框架。不同于仅预测业务负载或短期资源状态的现有方法，该框架借助世界模型（World Model）学习预算条件下的用户级 QoS 可行性动态，从而将难以直接求解的跨层混合整数优化问题转化为慢时间尺度预算控制与快时间尺度可行性感知调度的协同设计。在切片层，世界模型由后验网络、先验网络与强化学习决策智能体三者协同。后验网络融合 MAC 层反馈，对无线环境的演化动态进行建模，先验网络在缺乏实时观测时逼近后验以生成想象轨迹，强化学习决策智能体则在想象轨迹上学习带宽与功率预算策略，并通过与环境交互的在线后验校正持续修正想象。在 MAC 层，本文将无线资源调度由吞吐或紧迫度驱动重构为预算约束下的 QoS 可行性感知选择，基于服务质量类别标识（QoS Class Identifier，QCI）优先级的背包式调度器先将每条业务流转化为满足其 QoS 门限所需的最小资源块组（Resource Block Group，RBG）--功率可行项，再在切片层带宽与功率预算下按优先级加权选取可行项子集。该机制以优先级加权 QoS 满足率为优化目标，在资源受限条件下兼顾用户级 QoS 保障能力与无线资源利用效率。在 Starlink 规模化仿真中，DTPS 在过载业务场景下实现 $80.8\%$ 的用户级 QoS 满足率，显著高于代表性方法的 $33.0\%$--$56.3\%$。当业务突发强度变化时，QoS 满足率波动低于 $0.1$ 个百分点，说明该框架在非平稳业务条件下具有较好的调度稳定性。

（4）面向资源受限下的可靠传输与差错控制方面，LEO 卫星的端到端长传输时延与载荷计算受限性使语义编解码须在计算复杂度与传输效率之间权衡。现有高性能语义图像传输多依赖任务专用语义编码器、大参数量 Transformer 联合信源信道编码（Joint Source-Channel Coding，JSCC）或生成式复原模型，其计算、存储与时延开销难以适配星上受限载荷。为此，本文提出协同增量语义混合自动重传请求（Coordinated Incremental Semantic Hybrid Automatic Repeat Request，CIS-HARQ）机制，将每一轮 HARQ 重构为反馈引导的选择性语义精化过程，使轻量化收发机经多轮收发协同逐步逼近高复杂度模型的任务性能。在每一轮交互中，接收端在严格反馈预算下，依据各语义分量对任务判决的贡献度，定位对当前任务判决最关键的语义分量，并将其压缩为紧凑的 Top-$K$ 硬掩码反馈，经低速控制信令回传至发送端。发送端据此结合 HARQ 轮次嵌入，由反馈条件化增量编码器生成选择性语义增量，避免重复发送完整语义表征。接收端再以门控融合与掩码引导的语义累积机制仅更新被请求分量，在保留有用历史语义、抑制含噪与冗余增量的同时，于前向链路、反馈链路与模型复杂度三重约束下逐步恢复任务相关语义。在 ImageNet-100 数据集、信噪比 $5$ dB、信道带宽比（Channel Bandwidth Ratio，CBR）约为 $0.02$ 的紧带宽条件下，CIS-HARQ 经三轮增量传输将下游任务精度由同带宽基线的 $35.7\%$ 提升至 $65.0\%$，模型规模与推理时延均低于生成式基线，并在不同数据分布的数据集与多任务接收端上保持一致的精化增益。

综上，本文以分布式协同决策为主线，以状态可信与传输可靠为前提、以效率与公平的协同权衡为核心，构建了覆盖可信感知、公平接入、高效调度与可靠传输的闭环管控体系。四项关键技术并非孤立的分层优化，而是沿“感知—接入—调度—传输”路径形成“前提—核心”协同关系：一方面，可信网络状态感知与语义可靠传输分别在路径的输入侧与输出侧确立可靠性前提，前者保障路由决策所依赖的网络状态可信，后者以低开销为业务传输提供差错恢复，二者共同构成效率与公平优化的可信基础；另一方面，在此基础上，公平协作切换与双时间尺度预测调度协同求解效率与公平的核心权衡，前者在无全局协调下保障连续接入与多用户长期公平，后者将切片级预算转化为受限资源下的用户级 QoS 保障。星座规模仿真与语义传输实验表明，所提机制在网络状态可信感知、接入公平性、过载业务下用户级 QoS 满足率与紧带宽下任务精度等方面均取得显著增益。本文将大规模 LEO 星座资源管控由分层独立优化推进至面向端到端 QoS 的分布式协同管控，为 6G 空天地一体化网络的星上边缘自治提供理论方法与可验证的技术路径。

\end{abstract}

\begin{abstract}[en]
In the IMT-2030 vision of sixth-generation mobile communications (6G), low Earth orbit (LEO) satellite networks are emerging as key infrastructure for non-terrestrial access, inter-satellite backhaul, and global service continuity, owing to their wide coverage, relatively low transmission latency, and high capacity. With the advancement of miniaturized payloads and onboard computing capabilities, artificial intelligence (AI)-driven network control is evolving from ground-based offline optimization toward onboard online decision-making. However, unlike terrestrial cellular networks, which can perform centralized control by relying on a stable topology, reliable backhaul, and observable global states, LEO constellations are subject to inherent constraints such as high mobility, degraded telemetry, unreliable backhaul, and limited onboard resources, and therefore cannot directly adopt the terrestrial centralized paradigm. Accordingly, the control paradigm of satellite networks is shifting from ground-centric centralized scheduling toward onboard autonomy and local-neighborhood coordination. Under this trend, LEO constellations are no longer expected to serve merely as transparent forwarding relays. Instead, they must evolve into autonomous networks with quality-of-service (QoS) assurance capabilities to support end-to-end service provisioning in 6G space-air-ground integrated networks.

End-to-end transmission with QoS assurance requires the joint support of three dimensions: the reliability of network-state perception and service transmission, the efficiency of resource allocation under resource constraints, and the access fairness under multi-user contention. AI-based onboard distributed control, which replaces global-state synchronization and centralized computation with local decision-making and neighborhood coordination, offers a promising paradigm for this purpose; yet under decentralized control, these dimensions, projected onto the ``perception--access--scheduling--transmission'' path, concretize into four key difficulties intrinsic to LEO constellations. First, degraded telemetry makes the availability of local observations and the trustworthiness of restored states hard to reconcile, and the uncertainty of the restoration residual propagates along multi-hop aggregation, preventing nodes from building a trustworthy local multi-hop network view. Second, massive concurrent access without centralized coordination causes each user's short-term access gain to conflict with system-level long-term fairness and load balancing, which must be resolved online under limited neighborhood signaling. Third, a granularity--timescale mismatch exists between slow-timescale slice-level budgeting and millisecond-level fine-grained MAC-layer resource mapping, so that slice-level quotas can hardly be translated into user-level QoS assurance. Fourth, the absence of task priors at the transmitter, compounded by feedback, bandwidth, and computing constraints, makes it difficult to close the loop among low-overhead retransmission triggering, feature-level incremental encoding, and low-complexity semantic combining at the receiver. These four difficulties correspond to the assurance requirements of state reliability, access fairness, resource efficiency, and transmission reliability. Therefore, the core problem addressed in this dissertation is how to jointly guarantee reliability, efficiency, and fairness along the end-to-end transmission path in the absence of reliable global states and centralized coordination.

Building on local state calibration, limited neighborhood interaction, and cross-node cooperation, this dissertation develops a set of distributed cooperative resource-management mechanisms that can be executed online onboard satellites and reduce the dependence on global-state synchronization and centralized decision-making. Along the ``perception--access--scheduling--transmission'' chain, end-to-end QoS assurance is decomposed into four key techniques---trustworthy state perception, fair cooperative handover, slice--MAC cross-layer scheduling, and reliable semantic transmission---that jointly form a closed-loop resource-management framework for large-scale LEO constellations. The main contributions of this dissertation are summarized as follows.

(1) For robust network-state perception, stale and missing neighbor states contaminate the reliable information in state aggregation, weakening the reliability of routing decisions. To address this issue, this dissertation proposes a Confidence-Adaptive Local Distributed Processing (CA-LDP) framework that achieves dynamically adaptive graph-signal restoration and confidence-modulated graph-signal aggregation. Within CA-LDP, Confidence-Adaptive Graph Signal Restoration (CaGSR) casts the recovery of degraded telemetry as a credit assignment among three information sources, namely the temporal prior, the spatial correlation, and the latest observation, weighing spatio-temporal correlation against information freshness to calibrate stale and missing telemetry. To prevent low-confidence nodes from contaminating reliable nodes during information propagation, a confidence-weighted multi-hop propagation mechanism models state aggregation as a reaction--diffusion process on the graph, in which the node confidence controls the intensity of information diffusion: the sender-side confidence controls how strongly a node state diffuses to its neighbors, whereas the receiver-side confidence controls how strongly a node retains its own reliable state. Under nine controlled telemetry-degradation scenarios on a real Starlink constellation comprising 1584 satellites, CA-LDP improves the average transmission rate by $4.8\%$--$10.8\%$, reduces the end-to-end delay by $6.7\%$--$11.1\%$, and keeps the service delivery ratio close to $100\%$, compared with representative baselines. These results show that CA-LDP enhances the robustness of distributed routing under degraded telemetry.

(2) For mobility management toward service-continuity assurance, massive concurrent handovers driven by local observations cause individual user capacity maximization to conflict with system-level access fairness. To address this issue, this dissertation proposes a Fairness-Guided Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (FG-MAPPO) method, which converts the system-level fairness objective into an advantage correction term that each user can estimate locally, thereby enabling fairness-aware cooperative handover without global coordination. A Generalized Gini Social Welfare Function is adopted to jointly characterize throughput efficiency and access fairness, and a Multi-Objective Markov Decision Process (MOMDP) is formulated and its policy gradient derived, showing that each user only needs to broadcast its social-welfare-corrected individual advantage estimate within its neighborhood to enable low-overhead neighborhood fairness estimation. On this basis, a Throughput-Oriented and Fairness-Oriented (TOFO) network architecture and a two-stage training mechanism are designed to reduce the variance of multi-objective value estimation, and a distributed synchronous training framework compatible with the 3GPP Conditional Handover (CHO) procedure is developed. With only neighborhood information exchange, FG-MAPPO improves the Generalized Gini Fairness (GGF) metric by $3.48\%$ and the average rate by $6.91\%$ compared with a centralized Hungarian algorithm that uses global information. Compared with distributed reinforcement-learning baselines, it improves the handover success rate and the load-balancing performance by up to $15.65\%$ and $30.91\%$, respectively. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of local fairness estimation and distributed cooperative handover.

(3) For resource-efficient user-level QoS assurance, the dynamics of traffic and the network together with multi-user contention make the long-term system dynamics difficult to characterize, so that slice-level budgeting and MAC-level scheduling can hardly be coordinated and long-term user-level QoS is hard to guarantee. To address this issue, this dissertation proposes a Dual-Timescale Predictive Scheduling (DTPS) framework. Instead of existing methods that forecast only traffic demand or short-term resource states, DTPS employs an environment-evolution prediction model, or world model, to learn the budget-conditioned dynamics of user-level QoS feasibility, thereby transforming the intractable cross-layer mixed-integer optimization problem into a cooperative design between slow-timescale budget control and fast-timescale feasibility-aware scheduling. At the slice layer, the world model consists of three coupled components: a posterior network, a prior network, and a reinforcement-learning decision agent. The posterior network incorporates MAC-layer feedback to model the evolving dynamics of the radio environment. The prior network approximates this posterior in the absence of real-time observations and thereby generates imagined trajectories. The decision agent learns the bandwidth and power budgeting policy on these imagined trajectories and continually corrects the imagination through online posterior correction driven by interaction with the environment, keeping it close to the actual radio-environment dynamics. At the MAC layer, radio resource scheduling is recast from throughput- or urgency-driven allocation into budget-constrained QoS-feasibility-aware selection: a QoS Class Identifier (QCI)-priority-based knapsack scheduler first converts each service flow into the minimum resource block group (RBG)--power item required to meet its QoS threshold, and then selects a priority-weighted subset of feasible items under the slice-level bandwidth and power budgets. With the priority-weighted QoS satisfaction ratio as the optimization objective, the proposed mechanism improves both user-level QoS assurance and radio resource utilization under resource-constrained conditions. In Starlink-scale simulations, DTPS achieves a user-level QoS satisfaction ratio of $80.8\%$ under overloaded traffic, outperforming representative baselines that achieve $33.0\%$--$56.3\%$. When the traffic burst intensity varies, the fluctuation of the QoS satisfaction ratio remains below $0.1$ percentage points, which demonstrates stable scheduling performance under non-stationary traffic conditions.

(4) For reliable transmission and error control under resource constraints, the long end-to-end transmission latency and limited onboard computing of LEO satellites force the semantic codec to trade off computational complexity against transmission efficiency. Existing high-performance semantic image transmission usually relies on task-specific semantic encoders, large Transformer-based joint source-channel coding (JSCC) models, or generative restoration models, whose computation, memory, and latency costs are difficult to accommodate on resource-constrained satellite payloads. To address this issue, this dissertation proposes a Coordinated Incremental Semantic Hybrid Automatic Repeat Request (CIS-HARQ) mechanism, which reformulates each HARQ round as a feedback-guided selective semantic refinement process, allowing lightweight transceivers to progressively approach the task performance of high-complexity models through multi-round transceiver coordination. In each round, under a stringent feedback budget, the receiver evaluates the contribution of each semantic component to the task decision, locates the components most critical to the current task decision, compresses them into a compact Top-$K$ hard-mask feedback, and returns it to the transmitter over a low-rate control channel. Conditioned on this mask and the HARQ-round embedding, the transmitter generates selective semantic increments through a feedback-conditioned incremental encoder, thereby avoiding repeated transmission of the complete semantic representation. The receiver then applies gated fusion and mask-guided semantic accumulation to update only the requested components, preserving useful historical semantics while suppressing noisy or redundant increments, and thereby progressively recovers task-relevant semantics under joint forward-link, feedback-link, and model-complexity constraints. On the ImageNet-100 dataset, under an SNR of $5$ dB and a tight channel bandwidth ratio (CBR) of approximately $0.02$, CIS-HARQ improves the downstream task accuracy from $35.7\%$ for the equal-bandwidth baseline to $65.0\%$ after three incremental transmission rounds, while achieving a smaller model size and lower inference latency than the generative baseline, and maintaining consistent refinement gains across datasets with different data distributions and across multi-task receivers.

In summary, with distributed cooperative decision-making as the main thread, taking trustworthy state and reliable transmission as the prerequisite and placing the coordinated efficiency--fairness trade-off at the core, this dissertation builds a closed-loop framework covering reliable perception, fair access, efficient scheduling, and reliable transmission. Rather than isolated per-layer optimizations, the four techniques form a ``prerequisite--core'' relationship along the ``perception--access--scheduling--transmission'' path: trustworthy network-state perception and reliable semantic transmission establish the reliability prerequisite at the input and output ends---the former safeguarding the network state on which routing decisions rely, the latter providing low-overhead error recovery for service transmission---and together constitute the trustworthy foundation on which fairness-aware cooperative handover and dual-timescale predictive scheduling jointly resolve the efficiency--fairness trade-off, the former sustaining continuous access and long-term multi-user fairness without global coordination and the latter translating slice-level budgets into user-level QoS assurance under limited resources. Constellation-scale simulations and semantic transmission experiments show that the proposed mechanisms achieve significant gains in reliable network-state perception, access fairness, user-level QoS satisfaction under overloaded traffic, and task accuracy under tight bandwidth. This dissertation advances resource management for large-scale LEO constellations from layered and isolated optimization toward end-to-end QoS-oriented distributed cooperative management, thereby providing theoretical methods and a verifiable technical path for onboard edge autonomy in 6G space-air-ground integrated networks.
\end{abstract}
