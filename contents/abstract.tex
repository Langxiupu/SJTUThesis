\begin{abstract}[zh]
在 IMT-2030 描绘的第六代移动通信（6G）愿景中，低轨（Low Earth Orbit，LEO）卫星网络凭借广覆盖、较低传输时延与高容量等特性，正成为支撑非地面接入、星间回传与全球连续服务的关键基础设施。随着小型化载荷与星上计算能力的发展，人工智能（Artificial Intelligence，AI）驱动的网络管控正由地面离线优化向星上在线决策演进，卫星网络管控模式也由地面集中式调度逐步转向星上自治与邻域协同。在此趋势下，LEO 星座不再仅作为透明转发管道，而需演进为具备服务质量（Quality of Service，QoS）保障能力的自治网络，以支撑 6G 空天地一体化网络中的端到端业务承载。

面向 QoS 的端到端传输可概括为状态感知与业务传输可靠性、受限资源下的资源配置效率，以及多用户竞争下的接入公平性三类关键维度的联合保障。可靠的网络状态感知与业务传输是资源调度、路由决策和接入控制的基础，资源效率与接入公平性则构成资源受限条件下的主要权衡。地面网络凭借稳定拓扑、可靠回程与可观测全局状态，可以实施集中式跨层优化；而在大规模 LEO 星座中，高速运动、遥测退化、不可靠回程与星上资源受限使节点难以实时获得可靠的全局网络视图。同时，联合优化的决策空间随星座规模、用户规模与资源维度快速增长，集中求解结果又会因拓扑与信道的快速变化而迅速失效。因此，依赖全局状态同步与集中计算的跨层优化在星座尺度上既难以求解，也难以部署。如何在缺乏可靠全局状态与中心化协调的条件下，沿端到端路径协同保障可靠性、效率与公平性，构成本文研究的核心问题。

为此，本文提出一套可在星上在线执行的分布式协同资源管控方法体系，以本地状态校准、有限邻域交互与跨节点协作为基础，降低对全局状态同步与中心化决策的依赖，并沿“感知—接入—调度—传输”链路将端到端 QoS 保障分解为四项相互衔接的关键技术。网络状态感知与语义可靠传输分别从路由状态输入侧和端到端业务传输侧保障可靠性，协作切换与跨层调度在接入侧和资源侧协调公平性与效率，共同构成面向大规模 LEO 星座的闭环管控体系。本文的主要工作与创新点如下。

（1）在面向分布式路由的网络状态感知方面，退化遥测会使局部状态陈旧、缺失且置信度不一致，从而削弱路由决策的可靠性。为此，本文提出置信度自适应局部分布式处理（Confidence-Adaptive Local Distributed Processing，CA-LDP）框架，将校准后置信度贯穿状态校准与多跳聚合全过程，以克服现有方法中状态恢复与特征聚合相互解耦、残留状态不确定性在多跳聚合中扩散的不足。其中，置信度自适应图信号修复（Confidence-Adaptive Graph Signal Restoration，CaGSR）通过观测—先验参考机制校准陈旧与缺失遥测，并输出校准后置信度；置信度加权多跳传播机制则在发送端抑制弱支撑状态的向外扩散，在接收端锚定可靠的拥塞证据，使路由节点在利用局部观测的同时显式区分不同状态证据的可信程度。在 Starlink 壳层星座的九类受控遥测退化场景下，CA-LDP 相较代表性基线将平均传输速率提升 $4.8\%$--$10.8\%$，端到端时延降低 $6.7\%$--$11.1\%$，业务传输成功率保持接近饱和。结果表明，将置信度耦合于状态校准与多跳传播，可在退化遥测条件下增强分布式路由的鲁棒性。

（2）在接入与移动性管理方面，随机接入缺乏全局协调，海量用户并发切换会引发接入竞争与公平性失衡。为此，本文提出公平引导的多智能体近端策略优化（Fairness-Guided Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization，FG-MAPPO）方法，将系统级公平目标转化为可由用户本地估计的优势修正量，从而在无全局协调的条件下实现公平感知的协作切换。该方法以广义 Gini 函数（Generalized Gini Function，GGF）统一刻画吞吐效率与接入公平性，构建多目标马尔可夫决策过程（Multi-Objective Markov Decision Process，MOMDP），并推导出多目标策略梯度的解析形式；由此可证明，各用户仅需在邻域内广播经 GGF 修正的个体优势估计，即可低开销地估计邻域公平性。在此基础上，本文设计吞吐与公平分立（Throughput-Oriented and Fairness-Oriented，TOFO）网络结构与两阶段训练机制以降低多目标价值估计的方差，并构建与 3GPP 条件切换（Conditional Handover，CHO）兼容的分布式同步训练框架。仿真结果表明，仅交互邻域信息时，FG-MAPPO 的 GGF 公平性指标较具备全局信息的集中式匈牙利算法提升 $3.48\%$，平均速率提升 $6.91\%$；相较分布式强化学习基线，其切换成功率与负载均衡性能最高分别提升 $15.65\%$ 和 $30.91\%$。上述结果说明局部公平估计与分布式协作切换是有效的。

（3）在无线资源跨层调度方面，部分可观测、随机业务突发与时变服务能力使切片级预算控制与 MAC 级无线资源分配难以协同，用户级 QoS 因而难以保障。针对该问题，本文提出双时间尺度预测调度（Dual-Timescale Predictive Scheduling，DTPS）框架。不同于仅预测业务负载，该框架借助环境演化预测模型（World Model）学习 QoS 可行性状态的演化规律，从而将难以直接求解的跨层混合整数优化问题转化为慢时间尺度预算控制与快时间尺度可行性感知调度的协同设计。在切片层，先验—后验状态空间预测模型结合在线后验校正，在观测不完整与业务突发条件下生成具备前瞻性的带宽与功率预算决策；在 MAC 层，基于服务质量类别标识（QoS Class Identifier，QCI）优先级的 RBG--功率联合调度器将切片层预算转化为资源块组（Resource Block Group，RBG）与功率的联合分配，并以最小 RBG--功率可行性判定降低资源消耗。该机制以优先级加权 QoS 满足率为优化目标，在资源受限条件下兼顾用户级 QoS 保障能力与无线资源利用效率。在 Starlink 规模化仿真中，DTPS 在过载业务场景下实现 $80.8\%$ 的用户级 QoS 满足率，显著高于代表性方法的 $33.0\%$--$56.3\%$；当业务突发强度变化时，QoS 满足率波动低于 $0.1$ 个百分点，说明该框架在非平稳业务条件下具有较好的调度稳定性。

（4）在受限条件下的语义可靠传输方面，面向下游视觉任务的 LEO 卫星图像交付中，星上发送端通常仅负责图像获取与转发、对下游任务无先验（任务无关），而分类、识别等任务在地面接收端执行（任务感知）；现有高性能语义图像传输多依赖任务专用语义编码器、大参数量 Transformer 联合信源信道编码（Joint Source-Channel Coding，JSCC）或生成式复原模型，其计算、存储与时延开销难以适配星上受限载荷。为此，本文提出协同增量语义混合自动重传请求（Coordinated Incremental Semantic Hybrid Automatic Repeat Request，CIS-HARQ）机制，将每一轮 HARQ 重构为反馈引导的选择性语义精化过程，使轻量化收发机经多轮收发协同逐步逼近高复杂度模型的任务性能。在每一轮交互中，接收端在严格反馈预算下，将由任务输出裕量梯度刻画的任务相关语义缺口压缩为紧凑的 Top-$K$ 硬掩码反馈，并经低速控制信令回传至发送端；发送端据此结合 HARQ 轮次嵌入，由反馈条件化增量编码器生成选择性语义增量，避免重复发送完整语义表征；接收端再以门控融合与掩码引导的语义累积机制仅更新被请求分量，在保留有用历史语义、抑制含噪与冗余增量的同时，于前向链路、反馈链路与模型复杂度三重约束下逐步恢复任务相关语义。在 ImageNet-100 数据集、信噪比 $5$ dB、信道带宽比（Channel Bandwidth Ratio，CBR）约为 $0.02$ 的紧带宽条件下，CIS-HARQ 经三轮增量传输将下游任务精度由同带宽基线的 $35.7\%$ 提升至 $65.0\%$，模型规模与传输时延均低于生成式基线，并在不同数据分布的数据集与多任务接收端上保持一致的精化增益。

综上，本文围绕大规模 LEO 星座在缺乏可靠全局状态与中心化协调条件下的端到端 QoS 保障问题，以分布式协同决策为主线，构建了覆盖可信感知、公平接入、高效调度与可靠传输的闭环管控体系。四项关键技术沿端到端路径形成递进支撑关系：可信网络状态感知为分布式路由及上层调度提供本地多跳视图，构成在线决策的状态基础；公平协作切换为终端提供连续且公平的接入，支撑后续资源调度与业务传输；双时间尺度预测调度将切片预算转化为资源受限下的用户级 QoS 保障能力；语义可靠传输则在受限链路上以低开销提升任务相关信息的传输可靠性。星座规模仿真与语义传输实验表明，所提机制在网络状态可信感知、接入公平性、过载业务下用户级 QoS 满足率与紧带宽下任务精度等方面均取得显著增益。本文将大规模 LEO 星座资源管控由分层独立优化推进至面向端到端 QoS 的分布式协同管控，为 6G 空天地一体化网络的星上边缘自治提供理论方法与可验证的技术路径。

\end{abstract}

\begin{abstract}[en]
In the IMT-2030 vision of sixth-generation mobile communications (6G), low Earth orbit (LEO) satellite networks, owing to their wide coverage, relatively low transmission latency, and high capacity, are becoming a key infrastructure for non-terrestrial access, inter-satellite backhaul, and global service continuity. With the development of miniaturized payloads and onboard computing capabilities, artificial intelligence (AI)-driven network control is evolving from ground-based offline optimization toward onboard online decision-making, and the control paradigm of satellite networks is shifting from ground-centric centralized scheduling to onboard autonomy and local-neighborhood coordination. Under this trend, LEO constellations are no longer expected to act only as transparent forwarding relays, but need to evolve into autonomous networks with quality-of-service (QoS) assurance capabilities to support end-to-end service provisioning in 6G space-air-ground integrated networks.

End-to-end transmission with QoS assurance requires the joint support of three key dimensions: the reliability of network-state perception and service transmission, the resource allocation efficiency under constrained resources, and the access fairness under multi-user contention. Reliable network-state perception and service transmission provide the basis for resource scheduling, routing decisions, and access control, while resource efficiency and access fairness constitute the main tradeoff under resource constraints. In terrestrial networks, stable topology, reliable backhaul, and observable global states make centralized cross-layer optimization feasible. In large-scale LEO constellations, however, high mobility, degraded telemetry, unreliable backhaul, and limited onboard resources make it difficult for nodes to obtain a reliable global network view in real time. Meanwhile, the decision space of joint optimization grows rapidly with the constellation scale, user population, and resource dimensions, and centralized solutions may quickly become outdated due to fast-varying topology and channel conditions. Therefore, cross-layer optimization relying on global-state synchronization and centralized computation is neither tractable nor deployable at constellation scale. How to jointly guarantee reliability, efficiency, and fairness along the end-to-end path in the absence of reliable global states and centralized coordination constitutes the core problem addressed in this dissertation.

To address this problem, this dissertation develops a set of distributed cooperative resource-management mechanisms that can be executed online onboard satellites. Based on local state calibration, limited neighborhood interaction, and cross-node cooperation, the proposed mechanisms reduce the dependence on global-state synchronization and centralized decision-making. Along the ``perception--access--scheduling--transmission'' chain, end-to-end QoS assurance is decomposed into four interconnected key techniques. Network-state perception and reliable semantic transmission improve reliability at the routing-state input side and the end-to-end service transmission side, respectively, while cooperative handover and cross-layer scheduling coordinate fairness and efficiency at the access and resource-control sides. Together, they form a closed-loop resource-management framework for large-scale LEO constellations. The main contributions of this dissertation are summarized as follows.

(1) For network-state perception in distributed routing, degraded telemetry yields stale or missing local states with heterogeneous confidence levels, thereby weakening the reliability of routing decisions. To address this issue, this dissertation proposes a Confidence-Adaptive Local Distributed Processing (CA-LDP) framework, which carries post-calibration confidence throughout state calibration and multi-hop aggregation. This design overcomes the limitation of existing methods in which state restoration and feature aggregation are decoupled, causing residual state uncertainty to propagate during multi-hop aggregation. Within CA-LDP, Confidence-Adaptive Graph Signal Restoration (CaGSR) calibrates stale and missing telemetry through an observation--prior reference mechanism and outputs post-calibration confidence. A confidence-weighted multi-hop propagation mechanism further suppresses the outward propagation of weakly supported states at the sender side and anchors reliable congestion evidence at the receiver side. In this way, routing nodes can exploit local observations while explicitly distinguishing the credibility of different state evidence. Under nine controlled telemetry-degradation scenarios on a Starlink-shell constellation, CA-LDP improves the average transmission rate by $4.8\%$--$10.8\%$, reduces the end-to-end delay by $6.7\%$--$11.1\%$, and maintains a near-saturated service delivery ratio compared with representative baselines. These results demonstrate that coupling confidence with both state calibration and multi-hop propagation enhances the robustness of distributed routing under degraded telemetry.

(2) For access and mobility management, random access without global coordination can lead to severe access contention and fairness imbalance during massive concurrent handovers. To address this issue, this dissertation proposes a Fairness-Guided Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (FG-MAPPO) method, which converts the system-level fairness objective into an advantage correction term that each user can estimate locally. This enables fairness-aware cooperative handover without global coordination. Specifically, a Generalized Gini Function (GGF) is used to jointly characterize throughput efficiency and access fairness, a Multi-Objective Markov Decision Process (MOMDP) is formulated, and the analytical form of the multi-objective policy gradient is derived. The resulting derivation shows that each user only needs to broadcast its GGF-corrected individual advantage estimate within its neighborhood to enable low-overhead neighborhood fairness estimation. On this basis, a Throughput-Oriented and Fairness-Oriented (TOFO) network architecture and a two-stage training mechanism are designed to reduce the variance of multi-objective value estimation, and a distributed synchronous training framework compatible with the 3GPP Conditional Handover (CHO) procedure is developed. With only neighborhood information exchange, FG-MAPPO improves the GGF fairness metric by $3.48\%$ and the average rate by $6.91\%$ compared with a centralized Hungarian algorithm using global information. Compared with distributed reinforcement-learning baselines, it improves the handover success rate and load-balancing performance by up to $15.65\%$ and $30.91\%$, respectively. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of local fairness estimation and distributed cooperative handover.

(3) For cross-layer radio resource scheduling, partial observability, bursty traffic, and time-varying service capability hinder the coordination between slice-level budget control and MAC-level radio resource allocation, making user-level QoS difficult to guarantee. To address this issue, this dissertation proposes a Dual-Timescale Predictive Scheduling (DTPS) framework. Instead of predicting traffic load alone, DTPS employs an environment-evolution prediction model, or world model, to learn the evolution of QoS-feasibility states, thereby transforming the intractable cross-layer mixed-integer optimization problem into a cooperative design between slow-timescale budget control and fast-timescale feasibility-aware scheduling. At the slice layer, a prior--posterior state-space prediction model with online posterior correction generates predictive bandwidth and power budget decisions under incomplete observations and bursty traffic. At the MAC layer, a QoS Class Identifier (QCI)-priority-based RBG--power joint scheduler maps the slice-level budget into the joint allocation of resource block groups (RBGs) and power, and a minimum RBG--power feasibility test is used to reduce resource consumption. With the priority-weighted QoS satisfaction ratio as the optimization objective, the proposed mechanism improves user-level QoS assurance and radio resource utilization under resource-constrained conditions. In Starlink-scale simulations, DTPS achieves a user-level QoS satisfaction ratio of $80.8\%$ under overloaded traffic, outperforming representative baselines that achieve $33.0\%$--$56.3\%$. When the traffic burst intensity varies, the fluctuation of the QoS satisfaction ratio remains below $0.1$ percentage points, demonstrating stable scheduling performance under non-stationary traffic conditions.

(4) For reliable semantic transmission under resource constraints, in LEO satellite image delivery for downstream vision tasks, the onboard transmitter is typically responsible only for image acquisition and forwarding and has no prior knowledge of the downstream task (task-agnostic), whereas tasks such as classification and recognition are executed at the ground receiver (task-aware). High-performance semantic image transmission usually relies on task-specific semantic encoders, large Transformer-based joint source-channel coding (JSCC) models, or generative restoration models, whose computation, memory, and latency costs are difficult to accommodate on resource-constrained satellite payloads. To address this issue, this dissertation proposes a Coordinated Incremental Semantic Hybrid Automatic Repeat Request (CIS-HARQ) mechanism, which reformulates each HARQ round as a feedback-guided selective semantic refinement process, allowing lightweight transceivers to progressively approach the task performance of high-complexity models through multi-round transceiver coordination. In each round, under a stringent feedback budget, the receiver compresses the task-relevant semantic deficiency characterized by the task-output margin gradient into a compact Top-$K$ hard-mask feedback and returns it to the transmitter over a low-rate control channel. Conditioned on this mask and the HARQ-round embedding, the transmitter generates selective semantic increments through a feedback-conditioned incremental encoder, avoiding repeated transmission of the complete semantic representation. The receiver then uses gated fusion and mask-guided semantic accumulation to update only the requested components, preserving useful historical semantics and suppressing noisy or redundant increments, thereby progressively recovering task-relevant semantics under joint forward-link, feedback-link, and model-complexity constraints. On the ImageNet-100 dataset, under an SNR of $5$ dB and a tight channel bandwidth ratio (CBR) of approximately $0.02$, CIS-HARQ improves the downstream task accuracy from $35.7\%$ for the equal-bandwidth baseline to $65.0\%$ after three incremental transmission rounds, while achieving lower model size and transmission latency than the generative baseline, and maintaining consistent refinement gains across datasets with different data distributions and multi-task receivers.

In summary, this dissertation addresses end-to-end QoS assurance for large-scale LEO constellations in the absence of reliable global states and centralized coordination. With distributed cooperative decision-making as the main thread, it develops a closed-loop resource-management framework covering reliable state perception, fair access, efficient scheduling, and reliable transmission. The four key techniques provide progressive support along the end-to-end path. Reliable network-state perception provides a local multi-hop view for distributed routing and upper-layer scheduling, serving as the state basis for online decision-making. Fairness-aware cooperative handover provides users with continuous and fair access, supporting subsequent resource scheduling and service transmission. Dual-timescale predictive scheduling translates slice-level budgets into user-level QoS assurance under resource constraints. Reliable semantic transmission improves the reliability of task-relevant information over constrained links with low overhead. Constellation-scale simulations and semantic transmission experiments show that the proposed mechanisms achieve significant gains in reliable network-state perception, access fairness, user-level QoS satisfaction under overloaded traffic, and task accuracy under tight bandwidth. This dissertation advances resource management for large-scale LEO constellations from layered and isolated optimization toward end-to-end QoS-oriented distributed cooperative management, providing theoretical methods and a verifiable technical path for onboard edge autonomy in 6G space-air-ground integrated networks.
\end{abstract}
