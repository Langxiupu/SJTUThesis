\begin{abstract}[zh]
在 IMT-2030 面向的第六代移动通信（6G）体系中，低轨（Low Earth Orbit，LEO）卫星网络正由补充覆盖系统演进为支撑非地面接入、星间回传与全球连续业务承载的空间基础设施。随着再生式载荷、星间链路和星上处理能力成熟，LEO 星座的网络管控模式由地面集中配置转向星上自治决策与邻域协同。与地面蜂窝网络相比，大规模 LEO 星座受拓扑高速变化、业务时空分布非均匀、星间回传容量受限以及星上计算与存储资源有限等固有约束，难以沿用依赖全网状态汇聚和集中式资源调度的管控范式。面向 6G 空天地一体化网络，LEO 星座需要从透明转发型覆盖系统演进为具备分布式资源管控与用户级服务质量（Quality of Service，QoS）保障能力的核心承载网络。

用户级 QoS 保障不由单一链路或单一协议层独立决定，而取决于网络状态估计、终端接入控制、资源调度与跨层传输的连续协同。沿业务承载过程，用户级 QoS 保障可分解为网络状态感知、用户接入切换、无线资源分配与可靠业务传输四个子问题；从系统能力指标看，核心在于同时支撑通信可靠性、资源分配效率与接入公平性。在星上分布式管控条件下，退化遥测破坏本地多跳链路负载、队列积压与拓扑连通性视图，并使低可信状态在邻域聚合中扩散；多重覆盖与高动态可见关系增强接入切换竞争，长期欠服务用户难以保障接入连续性；切片层带宽-功率预算对未来 MAC 时隙级 QoS 可行域的保障能力难以前瞻预测，跨时间尺度资源粒度不一致削弱长期用户级 QoS 保障；星上轻量化语义传输难以在编码器复杂度、推理时延与传输性能之间取得平衡。本文提出面向大规模 LEO 星座的分布式协同资源管控方法，以本地网络状态校准、有限邻域信令交互和跨节点协作替代全网状态同步与集中式调度求解，并沿端到端业务承载过程设计四项关键技术：多源置信度调控的分布式网络状态感知框架、基于局部公平性估计的分布式协作接入切换机制、基于生成式网络态势感知的切片--MAC 分层协作优化框架，以及基于任务反馈与协同增量重传的轻量化语义传输机制。

（1）在网络状态感知方面，退化遥测会导致本地视图信息缺失，低可信状态在邻域聚合中的扩散又会造成邻居节点状态污染。本文提出多源置信度调控的分布式网络状态感知框架，即置信度自适应局部分布式处理（Confidence-Adaptive Local Distributed Processing，CA-LDP）框架。该框架将本地状态感知分解为状态校准与置信度约束传播：状态校准阶段联合译码观测、时序先验、邻域空间支撑和信息新鲜度，对退化遥测进行置信度自适应图信号修复；多跳传播阶段将校准置信度显式传递至路由特征构建过程，通过置信度加权聚合限制低置信状态的传播强度。分布式路由无需全局状态汇聚即可获得可信局部网络视图。在 1584 颗卫星的 Starlink 星座及三档负载、三档 BER 形成的九类受控遥测退化场景下，CA-LDP 相较代表性基线将平均传输速率提升 $4.8\%$--$10.8\%$，端到端时延降低 $6.7\%$--$11.1\%$，并维持接近 $100\%$ 的路由成功率。

（2）在用户接入切换方面，高动态多重覆盖使相邻终端在目标卫星、随机接入资源和子信道上形成强耦合竞争，导致长期弱服务用户的卫星连接难以持续维持。本文提出基于局部公平性估计的分布式协作接入切换机制，即公平性引导的多智能体近端策略优化（Fairness-Guided Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization，FG-MAPPO）方法。在线切换阶段，FG-MAPPO 以用户长期累计接入效用作为邻域信令，使终端低开销感知邻域长期服务状态；训练阶段采用广义 Gini 公平聚合函数构造公平性修正的训练信号，并通过吞吐优先--公平修正（Throughput-Oriented and Fairness-Oriented，TOFO）分立结构在保留链路质量判断的同时抑制长期服务分化。该协作训练与执行机制兼容 3GPP 条件切换（Conditional Handover，CHO）流程，可在仅依赖本地观测和有限邻域信令的条件下联合考虑候选卫星链路质量、负载状态和长期接入公平性。仿真结果表明，仅交互邻域信息时，FG-MAPPO 的广义 Gini 公平性指标较集中式规划参考提升 $3.48\%$，平均速率提升 $6.91\%$；相较分布式强化学习基线，其切换成功率与负载均衡性能最高分别提升 $15.65\%$ 与 $30.91\%$。该机制的邻域信令开销仅为 $7.73$ kB/s，可忽略。

（3）在无线资源分配方面，用户竞争、业务突发、信道波动等的网络不确定性导致反应式调度难以在网络变化前完成有效的资源调整。本文提出基于生成式网络态势感知的切片--MAC 分层协作优化框架，即双时间尺度预测调度（Dual-Timescale Predictive Scheduling，DTPS）框架。该框架以带宽与功率预算为跨层接口，将原始多星流级资源优化分解为单星切片预算与时隙级 QoS 可行流调度。切片层基于生成式网络态势感知和 MAC 反馈校正，学习候选预算下未来 QoS 违背率、服务速率、时延与资源占用的演化关系，以压缩未来 MAC 可行域所需的资源包络；MAC 层先估计候选业务流满足速率--时延联合门限所需的最小 RBG--功率开销，筛除 QoS 目标不可满足的业务流，再在可行流集合内执行 QCI 优先级加权背包选择。在 Starlink 规模星座仿真中，DTPS 在过载业务下实现 $80.8\%$ 的用户级 QoS 满足率，而 TDRL-RSUA、RL-JRSS、LACO 与 DQN-MAC 等基线为 $33.0\%$--$56.3\%$，并将平均时延保持在 $78.3$ ms；突发不确定性增强时，其 QoS 满足率始终维持在 $97.6\%$ 以上。

（4）在语义可靠传输方面，星上语义编/解码与恢复性能受到端到端传输时延和星上硬件开销限制，难以在多任务场景下高效恢复任务相关语义信息。本文提出基于任务反馈与协同增量重传的轻量化语义传输机制，即协同增量语义混合自动重传请求（Coordinated Incremental Semantic HARQ，CIS-HARQ）机制，将可靠性增益从模型复杂度扩展转向通信轮次与反馈协作。该机制将传统完整重传重构为任务反馈引导的选择性增量编码与跨轮合并：任务感知接收端根据当前接收语义状态与任务判别特征之间的差距生成反馈，星上发射端依据反馈生成受码率约束的语义增量，接收端再通过掩码引导的门控语义合并逐轮恢复任务相关特征。低开销实现包括基于任务判决裕量归因的语义分块价值排序、基于熵估计的增量码率约束和发射端基于 SFT 的反馈条件化编码。在 ImageNet-100、CIFAR-100 与 VeRi-776 数据集、Rician 衰落信道、信噪比 $5$ dB 且信道带宽比（Channel Bandwidth Ratio，CBR）约 $0.02$ 的紧带宽条件下，CIS-HARQ 经三轮增量传输将下游任务精度由同带宽基线的 $35.7\%$ 提升至 $65.0\%$；其以约 $2.2$ M 参数与毫秒级星上推理时延，参数量较生成式扩散接收端减少约 $2500$ 倍，任务精度差距控制在约 $7\%$ 以内。

综上，本文构建覆盖可信网络状态感知、公平用户接入、高效无线资源分配和低开销可靠语义传输的分布式闭环管控体系。四项关键技术沿端到端业务承载过程形成递进关系：可信局部状态视图支撑分布式路由，局部公平性估计约束接入切换，生成式网络态势感知评估切片预算对未来 QoS 可行域的保障能力，任务反馈与协同增量重传在受限链路和星上算力条件下恢复任务相关信息。星座规模仿真和语义传输实验表明，所提方法能在可靠全局状态缺失且地面实时控制闭环受限的条件下，改善网络状态感知可靠性、接入公平性、过载业务下的用户级 QoS 满足率以及紧带宽条件下的任务精度。本文将大规模 LEO 星座资源管控由分层独立优化推进至面向端到端 QoS 的分布式协同管控，并在有限邻域信令、可控星上计算预算和兼容 3GPP NR-NTN/CHO 流程的约束下，为 6G 空天地一体化网络中的星上边缘自治提供可验证的技术路径。
\end{abstract}

\begin{abstract}[en]
Under the IMT-2030 vision of sixth-generation (6G) mobile communications, low Earth orbit (LEO) satellite networks are evolving from complementary coverage systems into space infrastructure for non-terrestrial access, inter-satellite backhaul, and globally continuous services. As regenerative payloads, inter-satellite links, and onboard processing mature, network management in LEO constellations is shifting from ground-centric centralized configuration to onboard autonomous decision-making and neighborhood-level coordination. Compared with terrestrial cellular networks, large-scale LEO constellations face rapidly varying topology, nonuniform spatiotemporal traffic, constrained inter-satellite backhaul, and limited onboard compute-storage resources, making it difficult to inherit a management paradigm based on global-state collection and centralized scheduling. To serve as a core bearer network in 6G space-air-ground integrated systems, LEO constellations need to evolve from transparent forwarding systems to core bearer networks with distributed resource management and user-level quality-of-service (QoS) assurance.

User-level QoS assurance is not determined by a single link or protocol layer, but depends on continuous coordination among network-state estimation, terminal access control, resource scheduling, and cross-layer transmission. Along the service process, user-level QoS assurance can be decomposed into four subproblems: network-state awareness, user access handover, radio-resource allocation, and reliable service transmission. At the system level, it requires joint support for communication reliability, resource-allocation efficiency, and access fairness. Under onboard distributed management, degraded telemetry disrupts local multi-hop views of link load, queue backlog, and topology connectivity, and spreads low-confidence states through neighborhood aggregation. Multiple coverage and highly dynamic visibility intensify access-handover contention, making access continuity difficult for long-term underserved users. The ability of slice-layer bandwidth-power budgets to ensure future MAC slot-level QoS feasible regions is hard to predict, and inconsistent cross-timescale resource granularity weakens long-term user-level QoS assurance. Onboard lightweight semantic transmission struggles to balance encoder complexity, inference latency, and transmission performance. This dissertation proposes a distributed cooperative resource-management method for large-scale LEO constellations, replacing global-state synchronization and centralized scheduling with local network-state calibration, limited neighborhood signaling, and cross-node cooperation. Four key techniques are developed along the end-to-end service process: a distributed local network-state awareness framework with multi-source confidence regulation, a distributed cooperative access-handover mechanism based on local fairness estimation, a slice--MAC hierarchical cooperative optimization framework based on generative network situation awareness, and a lightweight semantic transmission mechanism based on task feedback and coordinated incremental retransmission.

(1) For network-state awareness, degraded telemetry disrupts local multi-hop state views, and low-confidence states may spread through neighborhood aggregation. This dissertation proposes a distributed local network-state awareness framework with multi-source confidence regulation, called Confidence-Adaptive Local Distributed Processing (CA-LDP). CA-LDP decomposes local state awareness into state calibration and confidence-constrained propagation. The state-calibration stage combines decoded observations, temporal priors, neighborhood spatial support, and information freshness to recover degraded telemetry through confidence-adaptive graph-signal restoration. The multi-hop propagation stage explicitly carries calibrated confidence into routing-feature construction, using confidence-weighted aggregation to limit low-confidence state propagation. Distributed routing thus obtains trustworthy local network views without global-state collection. In a 1584-satellite Starlink constellation and nine controlled telemetry-degradation scenarios formed by three traffic loads and three BER levels, CA-LDP improves the average transmission rate by $4.8\%$--$10.8\%$, reduces end-to-end delay by $6.7\%$--$11.1\%$, and maintains a routing success ratio close to $100\%$ over representative baselines.

(2) For user access handover, multiple coverage and highly dynamic visibility intensify access-handover contention, hindering access continuity for long-term underserved users. This dissertation proposes a distributed cooperative access-handover mechanism based on local fairness estimation, called Fairness-Guided Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (FG-MAPPO). During online handover, FG-MAPPO uses each user's long-term cumulative access utility as neighborhood signaling, enabling low-overhead sensing of long-term local service states. During training, generalized Gini fairness aggregation constructs fairness-corrected training signals, and the throughput-oriented and fairness-oriented (TOFO) structure preserves candidate-link quality judgment while suppressing long-term service differentiation. The cooperative training and execution mechanism is compatible with the 3GPP Conditional Handover (CHO) process and jointly considers candidate-link quality, satellite load, and long-term access fairness using only local observations and limited neighborhood signaling. With neighborhood information exchange only, FG-MAPPO improves the generalized Gini fairness metric by $3.48\%$ and the average rate by $6.91\%$ over a centralized planning reference. Compared with distributed reinforcement-learning baselines, it improves the handover success ratio and load-balancing performance by up to $15.65\%$ and $30.91\%$, respectively, with negligible neighborhood signaling overhead of $7.73$ kB/s.

(3) For radio-resource allocation, the capability of slice budgets to ensure future QoS feasible regions is hard to predict, and cross-layer resource-granularity mismatch weakens long-term user-level QoS assurance. This dissertation proposes a slice--MAC hierarchical cooperative optimization framework based on generative network situation awareness, called Dual-Timescale Predictive Scheduling (DTPS). With bandwidth and power budgets as the cross-layer interface, DTPS decomposes the original multi-satellite flow-level resource optimization into a single-satellite slice-budget subproblem and a slot-level QoS-feasible flow-scheduling subproblem. At the slice layer, generative network situation awareness with MAC feedback correction learns the evolution of future QoS violation ratio, service rate, delay, and resource usage under candidate budgets, compressing the resource envelope for the future MAC feasible region. At the MAC layer, QCI-priority weighted knapsack scheduling first estimates the minimum RBG--power cost required for each candidate flow to satisfy its joint rate--delay threshold, filters flows with unsatisfied QoS targets, and then selects resources within the feasible-flow set. In Starlink-scale simulations, DTPS achieves a user-level QoS satisfaction ratio of $80.8\%$ under overloaded traffic, while TDRL-RSUA, RL-JRSS, LACO, and DQN-MAC baselines reach $33.0\%$--$56.3\%$, and keeps average delay at $78.3$~ms. When burst uncertainty increases, its QoS satisfaction ratio remains above $97.6\%$.

(4) For reliable semantic transmission, onboard lightweight semantic transmission struggles to balance model complexity, inference latency, and transmission performance. This dissertation proposes a lightweight semantic transmission mechanism based on task feedback and coordinated incremental retransmission, called Coordinated Incremental Semantic HARQ (CIS-HARQ), shifting reliability gain from model expansion to communication rounds and feedback cooperation. CIS-HARQ reformulates conventional full retransmission as task-feedback-guided selective incremental encoding and cross-round combining. The task-aware receiver generates feedback from the gap between the current received semantic state and the task-discriminative features, the onboard transmitter generates rate-constrained semantic increments conditioned on this feedback, and the receiver progressively recovers task-relevant features through mask-guided gated semantic combining. Low-overhead implementation includes task-decision-margin attribution for semantic-block value ranking, entropy-based incremental-rate constraints, and feedback-conditioned SFT encoding at the transmitter. On the ImageNet-100, CIFAR-100, and VeRi-776 datasets over a Rician fading channel, at an SNR of $5$~dB and a tight channel bandwidth ratio (CBR) of about $0.02$, CIS-HARQ improves downstream task accuracy from $35.7\%$ for the equal-bandwidth baseline to $65.0\%$ after three incremental transmission rounds. With about $2.2$~M onboard parameters and millisecond-level inference latency, its parameter count is about $2500$ times lower than that of a generative diffusion receiver, while keeping the task-accuracy gap within about $7\%$.

In summary, this dissertation builds a distributed closed-loop management framework covering trustworthy network-state awareness, fair user access, efficient radio-resource allocation, and low-overhead reliable semantic transmission. The four techniques form a progressive service-path relationship: trustworthy local state views support distributed routing; local fairness estimation constrains access handover; generative network situation awareness evaluates how slice budgets ensure future QoS feasible regions; and task feedback with coordinated incremental retransmission recovers task-relevant information under constrained links and onboard computing budgets. Constellation-scale simulations and semantic-transmission experiments show that the proposed methods improve network-state awareness reliability, access fairness, user-level QoS satisfaction under overloaded traffic, and task accuracy under tight bandwidth when reliable global states are unavailable and real-time ground control loops are limited. Under limited neighborhood signaling, controllable onboard computing budgets, and protocol constraints compatible with 3GPP NR-NTN/CHO procedures, this dissertation advances resource management for large-scale LEO constellations from isolated layer-wise optimization to end-to-end QoS-oriented distributed cooperative management, providing a verifiable technical path toward onboard edge autonomy in 6G space-air-ground integrated networks.
\end{abstract}
